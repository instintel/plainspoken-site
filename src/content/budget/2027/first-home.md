*Updated Oct 11, 2026*

Stamp duty is a tax on the papers you sign when you buy a home: the document that moves the home into your name, and your home loan agreement. Budget 2027 keeps it at zero for first homes up to RM500,000, and halves it on the part of the price between RM500,000 and RM750,000. That covers agreements signed from Jan 1, 2027 to Dec 31, 2030.

## The RM500,000 waiver already existed. It now runs to 2030.

The full waiver on first homes up to RM500,000 has applied since Jan 1, 2021 and was due to end on Dec 31, 2027. Budget 2027 carries it on for agreements signed up to Dec 31, 2030.

## Homes up to RM750,000 now get part of the waiver too.

On a first home priced between RM500,000 and RM750,000, you pay no stamp duty on the first RM500,000 of the price, and half the usual stamp duty on the rest. Above RM750,000, the Budget gave no waiver.

So if the first home you're saving for costs a bit more than RM500,000, this is the part that's new for you.

## The government also stands behind home loans for 80,000 first-time buyers.

SJKP is a government scheme that guarantees home loans, so banks are willing to lend to buyers they might otherwise turn down, like people without a fixed monthly salary. Budget 2027 gives it up to RM20 billion in guarantees for about 80,000 first-time buyers.

## Buyers stuck in abandoned projects get a full waiver.

When an abandoned housing project gets revived, the original buyers and the contractors who rescue it pay no stamp duty, from 2027 to 2030.

## What happens next

- Jan 1, 2027: the new waiver window opens for agreements signed from that day
- Dec 31, 2030: the window closes
