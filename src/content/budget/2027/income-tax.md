*Updated Oct 11, 2026*

From income you earn in 2027, you can take RM12,000 off your income before your income tax is worked out, up from RM9,000. Two tax rates for middle earners also drop by one percentage point. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) says about five million taxpayers get up to RM1,600 a year more to spend from the two changes together.

## The basic relief goes from RM9,000 to RM12,000, the first change since 2010.

An amount the tax rules let you take off your income before tax is worked out is called a relief. This one is for every individual taxpayer, and it hasn't moved since 2010.

Taking RM3,000 more off means less of your income gets taxed. And the more you earn, the more that RM3,000 is worth to you.

## Two middle tax rates drop by one point, to 18% and 24%.

Malaysia taxes income in slices. The first RM5,000 is taxed at 0%, the next slice at 1%, and so on, and each rate only applies to the income inside its own slice. From 2027, two slices get cheaper:

- RM70,001 to RM100,000: from 19% to 18%
- RM100,001 to RM150,000: from 25% to 24%

Every slice below RM70,000 stays where it is.

## Income between RM1 million and RM2 million gets taxed at 30%, up from 28%.

That's the slice from RM1,000,001 to RM2,000,000. It now pays the same rate as income above RM2 million.

## For a middle earner, that's a few hundred ringgit a year.

The amount your tax is worked out on, after all your reliefs, is called your chargeable income. Same income, same other reliefs, using MOF's new rates:

- RM60,000 chargeable income today: the extra relief brings it to RM57,000, and the tax goes from about RM2,600 to about RM2,270, around RM330 less
- RM80,000 chargeable income today: the extra relief plus the new 18% rate take the tax from about RM5,600 to about RM4,960, around RM640 less

## Some new things count toward reliefs you already have.

Each relief keeps its cap, but more spending now counts:

- Lifestyle (up to RM2,500): AI software and app subscriptions, and adopting a pet from a registered shelter, plus its vaccination and sterilisation at a registered vet
- Sports (up to RM1,000): sports shoes, up to RM300
- Medical (up to RM10,000): postnatal care up to RM3,000, and breastfeeding equipment up to RM1,000
- Parents and grandparents (up to RM8,000): all kinds of care, not only medical care
- Courses (up to RM7,000): TVET courses for you or your spouse up to RM3,000, and STEM, arts, language or religious classes for your kids up to RM2,000

## It starts with the income you earn in 2027.

Everything here applies from the 2027 year of assessment, which covers what you earn from Jan 1 to Dec 31, 2027. You'll see it when you file your tax in 2028. Parliament still has to pass the Budget first.
