*Updated Oct 11, 2026*

From June 2027, the lowest monthly pay the law allows goes from RM1,700 to RM2,000, which is RM300 more before deductions, or about 18% more. The government says it covers over four million workers, but micro, small and medium businesses (MSMEs) with yearly sales below RM50 million are exempt while they adjust.

## The minimum wage is the floor no employer is allowed to pay below.

If you're paid RM1,700 a month today and you work at a bigger company, your basic pay has to go up to at least RM2,000 when the new floor starts in June 2027. The Budget named June 2027 but no exact day yet.

## If your employer is smaller, your pay may not change in June.

The exemption covers MSMEs with yearly sales below RM50 million. The government said it gives them time to adjust, and gave no end date for it. So whether your payslip moves in June depends on how big the business you work for is.

## Graduates and semi-skilled workers get a RM2,500 floor, as a start.

The Budget also set a separate baseline of RM2,500 a month for semi-skilled workers and graduates. The government called it a starting point and hasn't given a start date or the details yet.

## Government-linked companies lift their own pay benchmark to RM3,400.

Government-linked investment companies and the companies they own (GLICs and GLCs) use a living wage benchmark, a pay level meant to cover a basic decent life. It goes from RM3,100 to RM3,400 a month, covering about 230,000 workers. The government urged private employers to follow.

## What happens next

- June 2027: the RM2,000 minimum wage starts, outside the exempted MSMEs
- Not yet dated: details of the RM2,500 floor for graduates and semi-skilled workers
