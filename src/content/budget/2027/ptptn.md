*Updated Oct 11, 2026*

PTPTN is the government fund that lends students money for university and college. Budget 2027 lets borrowers earning up to RM2,500 a month put off their repayments in 2027. Those earning RM2,501 to RM3,000 pay at least RM50 a month, and borrowers who keep up with their payments get a 10% discount. The government says over 400,000 borrowers benefit from the pause.

## Earn RM2,500 a month or less, and you don't have to repay in 2027.

Putting repayments off like this is called a deferment. The loan doesn't disappear. You just don't have to pay during 2027, the only year this Budget covers.

## Earn RM2,501 to RM3,000, and the minimum is RM50 a month.

So a first jobber on RM2,800 a month can pay as little as RM50 a month.

## Pay regularly with nothing overdue, and you get 10% off.

Missed payments that are still owed are called arrears. If you have none and keep repaying on schedule, you qualify for the 10% discount on your repayment.

## Your employer can pay it for you and get a tax break.

Employers who repay their staff's PTPTN loans from Jan 1, 2027 to Dec 31, 2028 can claim it as a tax deduction, meaning they pay less tax on their own profits.

## You'll join EPF automatically at 18.

The Employees Provident Fund (EPF) is the retirement savings account most workers pay into from their salary. Under Budget 2027, every Malaysian citizen is registered with EPF automatically at 18, so the account is already there when your first job starts.

## What happens next

- Jan 1, 2027: the deferment year starts for borrowers earning RM2,500 or less
- Dec 31, 2028: the last day an employer's PTPTN repayments for staff count for the tax deduction
