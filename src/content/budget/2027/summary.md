*Updated Oct 11, 2026*

Budget 2027 is the government's plan for next year: how much it will spend in 2027, and where that money comes from. For your money, the biggest changes are lower income tax for middle earners and a RM2,000 minimum wage from June 2027. There's also more cash aid, and help with PTPTN, a first home and gig work. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, who is also the Finance Minister, presented it in Parliament on Friday, Oct 9, 2026.

## The government plans to spend RM459.8 billion in 2027.

That's up from RM441.1 billion in the Ministry of Finance (MOF)'s updated plan for this year. Most of it, RM376.8 billion, is the cost of running the country day to day: civil servants' salaries, subsidies, cash aid and the interest on what the government owes. The other RM83 billion goes to things that last, like roads and schools.

MOF also counts money that government-linked funds and companies plan to invest, plus projects it builds with private firms. With those added in, it puts the total at RM510 billion.

## It expects RM380.8 billion to come in, so it still has to borrow the rest.

That's what MOF expects to collect in 2027 from taxes, PETRONAS and other income, up 4.7% from this year. It's still less than the RM459.8 billion going out.

![The government plans to spend RM459.8 billion and collect RM380.8 billion in 2027 (Chart)](/images/budget/2027/chart-budget-2027-spend-vs-income-2026-10-11.png)

When a government spends more than it collects in a year, the gap is called a deficit. It covers that gap by borrowing. MOF wants the deficit at 3.3% of GDP in 2027, down from an estimated 3.6% this year.

GDP is the value of everything Malaysia produces in a year. So for every RM100 the whole economy makes, the government plans to borrow about RM3.30, a bit less than the RM3.60 it expects this year. MOF expects the economy to grow 4.2% to 5.2% in 2027, after 4.8% to 5.3% this year.

## More than RM80 billion goes to subsidies, aid and incentives.

When the government pays part of the price so you pay less, that's called a subsidy. About RM40 billion of that, or about half, is for fuel, the money that keeps BUDI95 petrol cheap at the pump. Cash aid through STR and SARA gets RM16 billion, up from RM15 billion this year.

## Six changes reach your own money directly.

- [Income tax](/budget/2027/income-tax/): the basic relief goes from RM9,000 to RM12,000, and two middle tax rates drop by one point, from income earned in 2027
- [Minimum wage](/budget/2027/minimum-wage/): RM1,700 goes to RM2,000 from June 2027, though smaller firms with yearly sales below RM50 million are exempt
- [Cash aid](/budget/2027/str-sara/): adults 18 and over who aren't on STR get RM100 of SARA credit twice in 2027
- [PTPTN](/budget/2027/ptptn/): no repayments needed in 2027 if you earn RM2,500 a month or less
- [First home](/budget/2027/first-home/): no stamp duty on a first home up to RM500,000 for agreements signed from 2027 to 2030, and half off part of it up to RM750,000
- [Gig work](/budget/2027/gig-workers/): a RM160 million package with Grab, plus top-ups of up to RM600 a year to your Employees Provident Fund (EPF) retirement savings

## None of it is law yet. Parliament debates it first.

What Anwar presented on Friday is a bill, a proposed law, called the Supply Bill 2027. Members of Parliament now debate it in stages, and each ministry's spending gets its own turn. Only after it passes does the money start flowing in 2027.

## What happens next

- Monday, Oct 12, 2026: Members of Parliament start debating the Budget, over eight days
- Oct 26 to 29, 2026: ministers reply to the debate
- Nov 2 to 26, 2026: each ministry's spending is debated line by line
- Jan 1, 2027: the new stamp duty window for first homes opens, and the 2027 tax year begins
- Around February 2027: the first RM100 SARA MADANI payment, before Hari Raya Aidilfitri
- June 2027: the RM2,000 minimum wage starts
