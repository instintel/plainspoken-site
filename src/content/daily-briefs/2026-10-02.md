*3-minute read*

Malaysia's exports jumped 45.5% in August on a year earlier, which is legit big. You'd think factories were flat out. Then factory managers filled in September's survey, and the vibe came back softer.

The S&P Global manufacturing PMI (a monthly poll of factory managers, where anything under 50 means more of them saw things get worse than better) slipped to 49.9 from 50.2. That's exactly where it sat in May, before three months of growth that peaked at 50.7. One caveat though. A PMI counts how many factories say things got better or worse, not how much they made. So "back to May" means the balance of factories is back where it was, not that output is.

![](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-02/malaysia-pmi-may-sep-2026-2026-10-02.png)

The soft patch was new orders, which fell for the first time in four months. Firms blamed sluggish demand at home, plus jammed ports and pricier fuel.

So both numbers can be true, and the gap sits in three places:

- different months (exports are August, the survey is September)
- different measures (export value includes prices, plus stuff like liquefied gas that never touches a factory order book)
- different customers, with overseas orders holding up while local ones slipped

For job hunters, factories hired at the fastest pace since April. Their costs also rose at the slowest pace since February. The KLCI still took an L, down 1.26% to 1,630.36, and a strategist quoted by Bernama pointed to the factory data and foreign selling.

If your business or side hustle sells both locally and overseas, compare each side's September sales with June. And if factories are hiring for orders that haven't shown up yet, how long can that last?

**Other things moving**

**China has stopped most of its fuel exports for October, and oil jumped back above US$100.**

Refiners there have suspended fuel shipments to pretty much everywhere except Hong Kong and Macau, Reuters reported, citing people briefed on the plan. Brent (the global oil price everyone quotes) settled at US$102.31 a barrel, up from US$98.03 a day earlier. When the Ministry of Finance (MOF) cut pump prices this week, strong Chinese fuel exports were among its reasons.

**South Korea just had its biggest export month ever, with chips doing the heavy lifting.**

Korea shipped US$120.9 billion worth of goods in September, a first for any month. For Malaysia, it's an early look at the regional electronics trade our own chip makers sell into, before our September trade figures come out. Reports put the jump down to AI spending pushing up memory chip prices.
