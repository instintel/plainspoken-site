*3-minute read*

Forecasters expected US employers to add about 90,000 jobs in September. They added 29,000. That's roughly a third, though the forecast is an average of economists' guesses and different surveys put it between about 84,000 and 95,000, so "about a third" is a rough guide, not an exact share. July was also revised to a month where the US lost 10,000 jobs.

![](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-03/us-new-jobs-jul-sep-2026-2026-10-03.png)

For you, this is a ringgit story. The Fed (the US central bank, which sets the main US interest rate, basically the price of borrowing there) raised it last month, and traders were betting on another hike on Oct 28. A week ago they put the chance at about 64%. After Friday's report it's roughly one in five. One US dollar has cost about RM4.08 all week, and Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) held its own rate at 2.75% at its Sep 3 meeting.

A Bank Muamalat economist said strong US data helped push up demand for the US dollar this week. Friday's data was the opposite, but it landed at 8:30pm, after our markets had closed. So the report can reach you in three places:

- the ringgit, if the US dollar loses some of that support
- the cost of borrowing in US dollars, which follows US rates
- nowhere yet, because Malaysian markets haven't traded on it

The second one did something odd. The US government's 10-year borrowing cost (the yearly interest it pays on 10-year government bonds) closed higher at 5.28%, from 5.24%, and nobody has a solid reason why. So no relief for US-dollar loans.

![](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-03/us-10-year-borrowing-cost-sep23-oct2-2026-10-03.png)

A hot reading on US prices on Oct 14 could bring the hike bets back. Will the ringgit notice on Monday?

**Also worth knowing**

**Rich economies agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel from emergency stocks.**

The G7 (seven rich countries, including the US and Japan) will start with diesel. France's president Emmanuel Macron said the move "should bring down prices". When the Ministry of Finance (MOF) cut our pump prices this week, it pointed to how much refined fuel was available worldwide, so this is the kind of change MOF watches.

**From Monday, you can't renew your road tax or driving licence through MyEG.**

The Road Transport Department (JPJ) is suspending MyEG and Zetrix AI as its collection agents, saying obligations under their agreements haven't been settled. Paying JPJ summonses through MyEG stops too. Road tax still works through the MyJPJ app, JPJ's website, its counters and kiosks, Pos Malaysia and Puspakom.
