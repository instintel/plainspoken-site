*3-minute read*

Over the weekend, seven big oil producers met online and changed nothing. They're the core of OPEC+ (the oil exporters' club led by Saudi Arabia and Russia), and they kept November's limits on how much each may pump where September's were.

You feel oil through fuel. Every week the Ministry of Finance (MOF) resets the price of unsubsidised RON95, RON97 and diesel, and the government pays the gap on BUDI95 and BUDI Diesel. When oil costs more, both the full price and the subsidy per litre tend to rise. They face the new oil price this week, right before Friday's Budget 2027 plans next year's subsidy spending.

Those limits aren't the problem though. Those seven pumped about 25 million barrels a day in August, roughly 5 million less than before the war, so about a sixth of their oil is missing. Both numbers are rounded estimates from news reports, not official OPEC figures, and the pre-war level is worked out from them, so "a sixth" is a rough guide.

The reason is the Strait of Hormuz, the sea lane that normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil and gas. Traffic has been sharply restricted since US-Iran fighting began on Feb 28, and on Sunday Iran said it stays closed until its conditions are met.

For now, Hormuz seems to matter more for supply than any OPEC+ meeting. A UBS analyst says the market "remains tight". Brent (the global oil price everyone quotes) ended Friday at about US$102 a barrel and restarts trading this morning.

A sharp price drop or a Hormuz deal would change that. Until then, this week's pump prices are the first test.

**Also this weekend**

From Nov 1, small gold bars brought into Malaysia will no longer carry a 10% import tax.

The duty goes to zero for minted bars from 1g to 50g. The national federation of goldsmiths and jewellers welcomed it, saying it "should improve affordability". If you save by buying small gold bars, this touches you directly, though nobody knows yet whether shop prices will actually drop.

**Vietnam's economy grew 9.95% in July to September, its fastest pace in four years.**

Factories and construction grew the fastest. Both Vietnam and Malaysia earn a lot from factory exports and foreign investment, so Vietnam is the neighbour to compare ourselves with. Malaysia's own number for the same three months isn't out yet.
