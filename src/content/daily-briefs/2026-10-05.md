*3-minute read*

Saudi Arabia, Russia and five other oil producers met online on Sunday and kept November's limits where September's were. They're the core of OPEC+, something like the the oil exporters' club, and it's the second month running with no change.

Meanwhile every week the Ministry of Finance (MOF) resets the price of unsubsidised RON95, RON97 and diesel, the fuel you pay full price for. The government also pays the difference on BUDI95 and BUDI Diesel, and that bill grows when oil costs more. Both will get tested once again this week, before Budget 2027 on Friday, Oct 9, which sets next year's fuel subsidy spending.

The OPEC+ limits wouldn't matter much right now, because these seven can't even get oil out effectively anyway. In August they pumped about 25 million barrels a day. Before the war, it was closer to the 30 million a day mark, so about a sixth of their oil is missing from the market. 

The ultimate hold-up is still the situation at the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow sea lane that normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil and gas. Ships have struggled to pass since US-Iran fighting began on Feb 28, and on Sunday Iran's parliament speaker said it stays closed until Iran's conditions are met. A UBS analyst says "the oil market remains tight".

Brent, the oil price everyone quotes, ended Friday at about US$102 a barrel and restarts trading this morning. A sharp drop or a Hormuz deal would change that. Until then, how much of the oil bill lands at the petrol pump, and how much in Friday's subsidy budget?

**Two more things from the weekend**

From Nov 1, small gold bars brought into Malaysia won't carry a 10% import tax anymore.

That covers minted bars from 1g to 50g, and the duty goes to zero. The goldsmiths' and jewellers' federation says the change "should improve affordability". If you save by buying small bars, this one's for you, though nobody knows yet whether shop prices will come down.

**Vietnam's economy grew 9.95% in July to September, its fastest pace in four years.**

Factory exports kept climbing and foreign companies put in a lot more investment. That's pretty much how Malaysia makes its money too, so Vietnam is the neighbour worth comparing ourselves with. Our own number for those three months isn't out yet.
