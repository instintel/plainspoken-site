*3-minute read*

When Malaysia's markets closed on Friday, one US dollar cost RM4.0820. Then the US reported weak jobs numbers. At Monday's 9am open the ringgit got a little stronger, to RM4.0805. By the 6pm close it was RM4.0845, a little weaker than Friday.

Tiny moves, but they land on anything you pay in US dollars, from a streaming subscription to goods for your online shop. The ringgit has stayed around RM4.08 since late September.

A Bank Muamalat economist linked the morning gain to the jobs news. Traders see roughly a 23% to 26% chance that the Fed (the US central bank) raises interest rates again on Oct 28, down from about 71% a week earlier. By the afternoon, though, the economist said the US dollar was gaining again because of worries about government debt in France and an early election in Spain.

The contradiction here is that fewer bets on a US rate rise didn't make borrowing in US dollars cheaper. The US government's 10-year borrowing cost, the yearly interest it pays to borrow for 10 years, ended Monday at 5.31%, up from 5.28% on Friday and about 4.2% at the start of the year. During the day it touched about 5.35%, the highest since 2002.

![](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-06/chart-us-10y-daily-2026-10-06.png) 

On day one, the jobs news reached Malaysians in three places:
- the ringgit, stronger in the morning and weaker by the close
- shares, with the KLCI (Malaysia's main share price scoreboard) up just 0.88 points
- US-dollar loans, with no relief

The next test for those rate-rise bets is US inflation on Oct 14, a reading of how fast prices are rising there. So far, nothing you pay or owe in US dollars has got cheaper.

**Elsewhere on Monday**

**Oil prices fell to about US$100 a barrel on Monday.**

Brent, the oil price everyone quotes, ended at US$100.32. Reuters shipping data showed Middle East oil shipments above pre-war levels on some days late last month, and rich countries plan to release emergency oil reserves. Oil prices feed into the weekly full price of RON95, RON97 and diesel set by the Ministry of Finance (MOF), with the next one due around Wednesday.

**Palm oil prices went up after six straight days of falls.**

Traders pointed to bargain hunting and higher US prices for soybean oil, a rival cooking oil. Palm oil for December delivery ended at RM4,578 a tonne. It's one of the big things Malaysia sells overseas, so the price matters to planters and smallholders. Traders are now waiting for the Malaysian Palm Oil Board's (MPOB) September count of palm oil in storage.
