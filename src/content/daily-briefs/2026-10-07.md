*2-minute read*

On Sep 18, the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) said August was Malaysia's best month ever for goods sold to the US. On Tuesday, the US's own count agreed, this time from the buyer's side.

Selling goods to other countries, called exporting, is a big part of how Malaysia earns money. In August, Malaysia's exports were 45.5% higher than a year earlier. So a record month of sales to the US means more money for the factories and firms that make those goods.

The US bought about US$8.6 billion of Malaysian goods in August. That's the most of any month this year, up from about US$5.1 billion in January. The US sells Malaysia less in return, and the difference, called a trade deficit, reached US$6.0 billion for the month. Reuters says that's the biggest goods deficit the US has ever run with Malaysia.

![](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-07/chart-us-imports-from-malaysia-2026-10-07.png)

Meanwhile, US companies bought a record amount from the whole world. Total US purchases from abroad hit US$420.8 billion in August, and their buying of equipment from abroad hit a record too, driven by chips and machinery for building AI systems. Electrical goods like chips are what Malaysia sells most of, though the US figures don't say how much of that chip shopping came from here.

The contradiction here is that the US has tariffs, an extra tax on goods coming in, and still bought a record amount. Economist Christopher Rupkey of FWDBONDS said the tariffs have done nothing to reduce how much the US relies on goods made abroad.

Still, that's one month. Malaysia's own September figures on Oct 19 will show whether US buyers kept up this pace or August was the top.
