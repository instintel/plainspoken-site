*2-minute read*

This week the government pays RM2.68 for every litre of RON95 you buy with BUDI95. You still pay RM1.99 at the petrol pump, so the government now covers 57% of that litre. Last week its part was RM2.53.

![](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-08/chart-budi95-government-part-2026-10-08.png)

That money comes from the federal Budget, the government's spending plan. Tomorrow at 3:30pm the government presents Budget 2027, which sets next year's spending on subsidies, the top-ups that keep your fuel cheaper.

Every week the Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets the unsubsidised price, the full price without BUDI95. On Wednesday it raised unsubsidised RON95 by 15 sen to RM4.67 a litre, the highest in the official weekly figures. RON97 rose 15 sen too, to RM5.15, and diesel stayed at RM5.27.

So last week's cut, to RM4.52 on Oct 1, didn't last. Back on Sep 3 the price was RM3.77, which makes it 90 sen more in five weeks.

If you pay the full price for RON95 or RON97, that's 15 sen more a litre. If you use BUDI95, nothing changes at the pump, and the extra 15 sen lands on the government instead.

MOF says the supply of refined fuel, the petrol and diesel that refineries make from crude oil, is under pressure abroad, partly because China stopped most of its fuel exports for October. Crude oil flows from West Asia are slowly recovering, MOF says. So more crude getting through may not bring the weekly price down quickly.

A cut next week would shrink the government's part again. So when Budget 2027 lands tomorrow afternoon, how much of each litre is the government planning to keep paying?

**One more thing from overnight**

**Most US central bank officials expect one more interest rate rise this year.**

That's in the record of the US Federal Reserve's September meeting, released on Wednesday US time. The Fed sets US interest rates, and its officials also agreed prices there are still rising too fast. Higher US interest rates keep borrowing in US dollars expensive, which matters for Malaysian businesses or families with loans or bills in US dollars.
