*2-minute read*

It's Budget day. The government presents Budget 2027 at 3:30pm, with oil back above US$100. That's its spending plan for next year, including what it spends keeping your fuel cheap.

Brent, the global oil price everyone quotes, ended Thursday at about US$104 a barrel, up about 4% in a day. US media had reported possible new US strikes on Iran, and a hurricane stopped some US oil production in the Gulf of Mexico.

On Wednesday the Ministry of Finance (MOF) said Brent stayed near US$100 in the days it used to set this week's fuel prices. Unsubsidised RON95, the full price without BUDI95, is RM4.67 a litre this week. BUDI95 stays at RM1.99, so the government pays the other RM2.68 on every BUDI95 litre.

So if oil holds around US$104, next week's full price faces pressure to go up. With BUDI95 fixed, any rise lands on people paying the full price and on the government's part of each litre. MOF also looks at the price of refined fuel, the petrol and diesel refineries make, so crude alone doesn't decide it.

Oil gave back some of the jump after President Trump wrote that the US won't attack Iran "at any time prior to" the US midterm elections on Nov 3. For now, the risk has a date attached.

The KLCI, Malaysia's main share price scoreboard, ended the same day at 1,601.01, its lowest finish this year, almost 5% down since January. An IPPFA analyst named higher energy prices, worries over conflicts abroad and foreign investors selling.

At 3:30pm today the Budget shows the government's side. The next weekly fuel price, due around Wednesday, shows the petrol pump side.

**Also worth knowing**

**A report that OpenAI earns less than people thought knocked the shares of big chip companies.**

The Financial Times reported on Thursday that OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, is on track for about US$50 billion in sales a year, well below what other reports said last month. Shares of the world's biggest chipmakers fell. No clear line to Malaysia yet, but worth knowing because spending on AI has been driving the chip boom.
