*3-minute read*

Malaysia ended September with 3.51 million tonnes of palm oil sitting in storage, the most ever recorded. Yet on Monday, the day that figure came out, the price of palm oil went up.

The count comes from the Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB), which tallies every month how much palm oil the country makes, sells abroad and keeps in its tanks. A month earlier the stockpile was 2.82 million tonnes, so it jumped by about a quarter in one month, the sixth monthly rise in a row.

![Malaysia's palm oil stockpile hit a record in September (Chart)](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-13/chart-palm-oil-stocks-2026-10-13.png)

This matters beyond the plantations. Palm oil is one of Malaysia's biggest exports, earning the country RM112.43 billion in 2025. Oil sitting in a tank hasn't been sold yet, so it hasn't turned into export money yet.

Two things built the pile. Plantations produced 2.12 million tonnes in September, the most in any month on record. Meanwhile exports fell 12.1% to 1.12 million tonnes.

Still, the price buyers agree today for palm oil delivered in December rose RM45 on Monday to RM4,637 a tonne, about 1%. Traders and news agencies pointed to shipments in the first ten days of October, estimated at about a third higher than the same days in September.

That's the part to keep an eye on. With storage at a record, those early-October shipments are what's holding the price up for now. If buying slows again, the pile is still there. Malaysia's September trade figures on Oct 19 will show what the slower month did to palm oil export earnings in ringgit. So which wins out, the record pile or the buyers abroad?

## Also moving today

**The global oil price had its highest close in almost a month.**

Brent, the oil price most of the world quotes, ended Monday at US$106.65 a barrel, its highest since Sep 15. It rose after weekend attacks on Riyadh's airport in Saudi Arabia and new strikes on tankers near the Gulf. Here, oil prices feed into the weekly full price of petrol and diesel, and into what the government pays to keep BUDI95 cheap.

**Genting's share price fell by almost a quarter after it asked its own shareholders for more money.**

The shares closed Monday at RM1.45, from RM1.88 on Friday, the steepest one-day fall since 1998. The group plans to sell new shares to its existing owners at a discount, mostly to pay down debt. If you own Genting shares, you either put in more money for the new ones or end up owning a smaller slice.
