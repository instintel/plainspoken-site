*3-minute read*

At 8:30pm Malaysian time on Wednesday, after Malaysia's share market and the ringgit had closed, the US said its prices rose 3.7% in the year to September. That's US inflation, how fast the cost of everyday things goes up, and it was 3.4% in August.

That matters here because of the US Federal Reserve (the Fed, the US central bank, which sets the main US interest rate, the price of borrowing money there). It raised that rate in September, to 3.75% to 4.00%, and decides again on Oct 28, US time. Traders' bets on another rise at that meeting went from about one in five to between a third and a half. Higher US rates tend to lift the US dollar, which means more ringgit for anything you pay or borrow in US dollars.

Most of the jump came from petrol. US petrol cost 34.2% more than a year earlier, and petrol alone made up over half of the rise in prices from August, according to the US office that counts them.

Still, take food and energy out and US prices rose 2.6%, a bit more than forecasters expected. That keeps another rate rise possible.

This year US inflation went from 2.4% in January to a high of 4.2% in May, then went down to 3.4% by August. September is the first climb since May.

Meanwhile the ringgit got slightly stronger on Wednesday, ending at 4.0805 to the US dollar from 4.0835. If the ringgit holds firm today and the Fed keeps its rate where it is, Wednesday's jump in the bets will have been a one-off. If not, how much more ringgit will your next US-dollar payment need?

## Two more things this week

**More fuel is coming into the region, so unsubsidised diesel costs 10 sen less a litre from today.**

Diesel in Peninsular Malaysia is RM5.17 a litre until Oct 21, down from RM5.27, and unsubsidised petrol gets a little cheaper too. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) said China is allowing fuel exports again this month and South Korea is still supplying the region, though crude oil stays expensive. BUDI95 drivers pay the same as before.

**China's exports grew 36.3% in September, far faster than forecasters expected.**

That's what China sold to other countries, compared with September last year. It pushes China's trade surplus, how much more it sells than it buys, towards a record, and its trading partners are putting pressure on it. No clear line to Malaysia yet. Malaysia's own September trade numbers land on Monday, Oct 19.
