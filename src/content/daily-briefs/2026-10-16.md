*3-minute read*

If you're paying off a home loan on a floating rate, your monthly instalment is built on the OPR, the main interest rate Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) sets and banks use to price your loans. BNM kept it at 2.75% at its last decision on Sep 3, and on Thursday BNM's governor said it could move.

![Photo of a hand holding a set of house keys.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-16/photo-opr-2026-10-16.jpg)

On Bloomberg TV in Bangkok, Governor Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour said BNM is "very vigilant" about prices going up, and has "space for us to act accordingly" if they keep rising. That means BNM could raise the OPR. He also said today's 2.75% still fits the outlook for prices and growth, so no decision has been made.

Meanwhile, traders are already leaning one way. Traders' bets tracked by Bloomberg put about a 76% chance on a BNM rate rise within six months, or roughly three in four. Asked about that, the governor said: "That's what the market is saying."

For now, prices in Malaysia are rising slowly. Inflation, how fast the cost of everyday things goes up, was 1.9% in August, inside the government's 1.5% to 2.5% range for this year. Still, the governor named a long Middle East conflict and high energy prices as risks, and inflation is expected to pick up in early 2027.

If BNM does raise the OPR, monthly instalments on floating-rate home and business loans would go up, and the interest banks pay on your savings would tend to rise. If September's inflation comes in below August's 1.9%, the case for a rise gets weaker.

BNM's last rate decision of the year is on Thursday, Nov 5. How much of your monthly instalment moves with the OPR?
