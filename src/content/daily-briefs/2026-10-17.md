*3-minute read*

Malaysia's GDP, the total value of everything the country's businesses and workers produce, grew 6.2% from October to December last year, then 5.4%, then 6.0% from April to June, each compared with the same three months a year earlier. On Friday, the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) put July to September at 5.3%.

![Malaysia's growth slowed to 5.3% from July to September (Chart)](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-17/chart-malaysia-gdp-growth-2026-10-17.png)

So growth slowed, but by less than many forecasters had guessed. Their estimates in a Bloomberg survey ran from 4.5% to 5.4%, so 5.3% landed near the top. For the first nine months of 2026, the economy grew 5.5%. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) expects 4.8% to 5.3% for the whole year, the forecast Budget 2027 was built on. So growth so far is running above the top of the government's own range. The jobless rate was 2.9% in August, so about three in every 100 people who wanted a job didn't have one.

Factories did the most growing. Manufacturing grew 7.5%, led by electronics. Construction grew 6.0%, and services, which covers shops, telcos and banks, grew 5.4%. Mining shrank 4.9% because Malaysia produced less oil.

Economists like Prof Yeah Kim Leng of Sunway University say the economy is on track to finish the year above MOF's range. Whether factories kept that pace shows on Monday, in September's figures for what Malaysia sold abroad.

The same day, Malaysia's main share index, the KLCI, which tracks the biggest companies listed on Bursa Malaysia, fell 0.98% to 1,607.76.

This 5.3% is DOSM's first estimate. The final figure comes out on Wednesday, Nov 18, and a big cut there would mean the economy isn't as far ahead of MOF's forecast as it looks today.

## Elsewhere on Friday

**Oil climbed to nearly US$110 a barrel as hopes of extra supply faded.**

Brent, the oil price everyone quotes, ended Friday at US$109.86. Bloomberg pointed to fading hopes of more oil arriving soon, Houthi rebels firing at Saudi Arabia, and Russia and Ukraine hitting each other's energy sites. Oil at that level feeds into next week's unsubsidised petrol and diesel prices, and into what the government pays on every BUDI95 litre.

**Indonesia's share market fell about 11% after MSCI proposed moving it off its main list of developing-country markets.**

MSCI's lists guide where many big global funds invest. MSCI said it has "significant concerns" about investing in Indonesian shares and will decide by the end of June 2027. It was Indonesia's biggest one-day fall since 1998. There's no clear line to Malaysia yet, but Indonesia is Southeast Asia's biggest economy.
