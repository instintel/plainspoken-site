*3-minute read*

Malaysian companies sold RM215.1 billion of goods to buyers in other countries in September. That's 54.5% more than the RM139.3 billion of September last year. The Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) says it's the first time Malaysia's exports have passed RM200 billion in a single month.

That money pays for factory work across the country. It also lands in the last month of July to September, when the whole economy grew 5.3% on the first estimate from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM). OCBC's economists say Monday's trade figures suggest that 5.3% could be revised up.

Since April, the monthly total has been RM183.3 billion, RM183.9 billion, RM177.9 billion, RM193.6 billion and then August's RM191.0 billion. August had looked like it might be the top. It wasn't.

![Malaysia's exports passed RM200 billion in September (Chart)](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-20/chart-malaysia-monthly-exports-2026-10-20.png)

Electronics led the way. Electrical and electronic products rose about 68%, and made up more than half of all exports. Factories also brought in nearly 70% more parts and materials from overseas than a year earlier, the bits that go into the goods they make.

From January to September, exports are up 34%. When the Ministry of Finance (MOF) presented Budget 2027 on Oct 9, it projected 31% for the whole year. To get there, the last three months need roughly RM179 billion a month.

Still, part of the jump is price. Exports are counted in ringgit, so higher prices make the number bigger even if Malaysia didn't ship more. MBSB Investment Bank says export prices rose 7.8% on average from April to August, and it expects trade growth to slow towards the end of the year.

October's figures, out in November, will show whether that slowdown has started.

## Also moving on Monday

**Prices in Malaysia rose 1.8% in the year to September, a little slower than August.**

DOSM's inflation figure, how fast prices go up, came down from 1.9%, partly because electricity costs for bigger household users rose less. Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) said on Oct 15 it has room to act on the OPR, the main interest rate behind your loans, if prices pick up. Its last decision of the year is on Thursday, Nov 5.

**China's economy grew 4.5% from July to September, faster than forecasters expected.**

That compares with the same three months a year earlier, and it's up from 4.3% in the quarter before. China is Malaysia's biggest trading partner, so how fast it grows matters to Malaysian firms that sell there.

**Indonesia's share market fell another 3.57% on Monday as the index company MSCI weighs a downgrade.**

MSCI is deciding whether to move Indonesia out of its main group of developing markets, and the rupiah weakened too. Bank Muamalat's chief economist said the effect on other currencies in the region is "fairly contained" so far. The ringgit actually got stronger against the rupiah on Monday.
