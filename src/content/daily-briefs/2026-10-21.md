*3-minute read*

A month ago, fewer than half of the economists in Reuters' monthly poll thought the US Federal Reserve would raise its interest rate again in December. In the poll out on Tuesday, all 100 of them did.

![Photo of a person carrying a strapped stack of books, seen from the waist down.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-21/photo-federal-reserve-2026-10-21.jpg)

The Fed is the US central bank, and its rate is the base cost of borrowing in US dollars. When it goes up, the US dollar tends to get stronger, so each dollar costs you more ringgit. That reaches anything you pay for or borrow in US dollars, from imported goods to fees for studying overseas.

Next week looks like a pause, though. Of the 100, 94 expect no change when the Fed announces early on Thursday, Oct 29, Malaysian time. Traders who bet on US rates agree, putting the chance of a December rise at about nine in ten.

Since a rise on Sep 16, the Fed's rate has been 3.75% to 4.00%. One more quarter-point rise in December would take it to 4.00% to 4.25%, where most of the economists think it stops.

Meanwhile, the ringgit didn't move, closing on Tuesday at 4.0805 to the US dollar, the same as Monday. Bank Muamalat's chief economist said investors are holding back before the Fed meets, and the expected December rise could explain the narrow range.

Malaysia's own main rate, the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) set by Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM), has been 2.75% since July 2025. BNM decides next on Thursday, Nov 5.

With a pause this widely expected, what the Fed says on Oct 29 about December may matter more than the decision. A surprise rise next week would change that. So does the ringgit stay this quiet as December nears?

## Elsewhere on Tuesday

**The global oil price climbed above US$111 a barrel on Tuesday, its highest since May, then fell back.**

Brent, the oil price everyone quotes, jumped after Yemen's Houthi group claimed attacks on Saudi energy sites, which Saudi authorities hadn't confirmed. Hopes of US talks with Iran pulled it back down. Oil feeds the full price of petrol and diesel here, and the Ministry of Finance (MOF) announces next week's prices this evening.

**Shares in South Korea's big chipmakers fell again on Tuesday, because of worries about the money going into AI.**

South Korea's main share index dropped 3.73%, with Samsung Electronics and SK hynix down. A Daishin Securities analyst said doubts over whether AI spending will pay off, and over the debt behind it, haven't gone away. Electronics were more than half of what Malaysia sold abroad in September, so chips matter for factory jobs here too.
