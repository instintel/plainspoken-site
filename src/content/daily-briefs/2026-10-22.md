*3-minute read*

If you pay the full price for RON95, a litre costs RM4.32 from today, 30 sen less than last week. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) announced it on Wednesday night, and it's the biggest one-week cut this year. RON97 also drops 30 sen to RM4.80, and full-price diesel in Peninsular Malaysia drops 10 sen to RM5.07.

If you're on BUDI95, nothing changes at the petrol pump. You still pay RM1.99, up to your 300 litres a month. The government pays the rest of each litre, and that part is now RM2.33, or 54% of the full price, down from RM2.63 (57%) last week. Top-ups like this are what Budget 2027's fuel subsidy money pays for, with about RM40 billion planned for next year.

Thirty sen is a lot for one week, but small against the climb before it. The full price was RM3.77 on Sep 3 and reached RM4.67 by Oct 8. So it has given back 35 sen of that 90 sen rise, and still sits about 55 sen above early September.

![The full RON95 price fell 30 sen this week, to RM4.32 (Chart)](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-22/chart-ron95-full-price-2026-10-22.png)

Still, oil itself didn't get cheaper this week. Brent, the global oil price everyone quotes, ended Wednesday at US$108.82 a barrel, up 3 cents. On the same day Iran said the Strait of Hormuz, a sea route for oil tankers, won't reopen to everyone until Iran reaches a deal with the US.

MOF's formula follows the average price of fuel in the week before, so this week's cut doesn't promise anything about the next one. If Brent stays near US$109, will next Wednesday's price fall again?

## Other things worth knowing

**Westports made 26% more profit from July to September, helped by higher port charges.**

Westports runs container terminals at Port Klang. Its profit for the quarter rose on higher port charges and more income from extra services, and it expects to handle at least as many containers this year as last year. Those port charges are part of what Malaysian importers and exporters pay to move goods.

**Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim wants to tackle the high commissions that online platforms charge small businesses.**

He named high commissions, strict contract terms and platforms' control of user data as burdens on small businesses on Wednesday, after chairing the government's digital economy council. No measure or date has been announced. If you sell online, the cut the platform takes from each sale is now on the government's list.

**The ringgit got a little stronger on Wednesday, ending at 4.0790 to the US dollar.**

That's from 4.0805 on Tuesday, a very small move. Bank Muamalat's chief economist linked it to the government keeping its finances in order while Budget 2027 spends to support growth. A stronger ringgit makes anything priced in US dollars a little cheaper for you, from imported goods to overseas fees.

**Meta, the company behind Facebook and Instagram, is sticking with plans to spend US$135 billion to US$145 billion this year.**

Meta gave the figure with its results after the US market closed on Wednesday and plans to keep investing heavily in AI in 2027. Electronics were more than half of what Malaysia sold abroad in September, the kind of hardware AI data centres buy. No figure shows how much of that spending reaches Malaysia.
