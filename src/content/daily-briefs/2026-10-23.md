*3-minute read*

Thursday was the first day of the cheaper RON95 price: RM4.32 a litre if you pay the full price, after a 30 sen cut. It was also the day oil jumped. Brent, the world's main oil price yardstick, ended Thursday at about US$113 a barrel, up about 4% from US$108.82 on Wednesday and its highest since May.

Oil matters because of how Malaysia sets fuel prices. Every Wednesday the Ministry of Finance (MOF) resets the full price of petrol and diesel, and its formula looks at the average price of the week before. On BUDI95, within 300 litres a month, you still pay RM1.99. The government covers the rest, RM2.33 a litre this week, or 54% of the full price. So a higher full price lands on anyone paying it, diesel businesses included, and on the government, which plans to spend about RM40 billion on fuel subsidies next year under Budget 2027.

The jump came after attacks claimed by the Houthi group hit an airport and an Aramco oil field in Saudi Arabia (Aramco said the field kept running). The US also told some diplomats to leave the country, and The New York Times reported US and Israeli plans for strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections on Nov 3.

Brent has climbed from about US$100 on Oct 7 and about US$106 on Oct 15. This week it's averaging around US$110 so far, about 3% more than last week's roughly US$107, with Friday still to come.

So this week's big cut says little about the next price, which MOF announces on Wednesday, Oct 28. If Brent holds near US$113 through Friday, how much room is left for another cut?

## More from Thursday

**Palm oil prices should stay above RM4,600 a tonne in November, says the Malaysian Palm Oil Council (MPOC).**

MPOC, the industry's promotion body, said on Thursday it expects more palm oil to be shipped abroad while output falls with the season. It also expects the stockpile in Malaysia to start shrinking from November. Palm oil is one of Malaysia's big exports, so its price decides what planters and smallholders get paid.

**Worries about the cost of AI spending hit tech and chip shares around the world on Thursday.**

In the US, the Nasdaq, the share index packed with tech companies, fell 2.6%, and Meta, which owns Facebook and Instagram, dropped sharply after its results. Electronics make up about half of what Malaysia sells abroad, the same kind of chips and hardware that AI spending buys, so doubts about that spending matter for factory orders here.

**Investors rushed to buy US government bonds, and the 10-year US interest rate fell back below 5%.**

That was the rate's biggest one-day fall since August 2024, as money moved into safer bonds while AI-linked shares dropped. This rate is basically what the US government pays to borrow for 10 years. US rates pull on the US dollar and the ringgit, and the US Federal Reserve announces its next decision on Oct 29, Malaysian time.

**China's main share index is now about 20% below its June peak.**

The CSI 300, which tracks China's biggest listed companies, fell again on Thursday, and a drop that size from a peak is what markets call a bear market. Shares fell across Asia the same day, though Bursa Malaysia's main index ended a little higher. China is one of Malaysia's biggest trading partners.
