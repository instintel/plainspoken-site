*3-minute read*

Brent, the world's main oil price yardstick, has ended every Friday this month higher than the one before:

- Oct 2: about US$102 a barrel
- Oct 9: about US$105
- Oct 16: about US$110
- Oct 23: US$116.72

![Brent crude closed higher three Fridays in a row (Chart)](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-24/chart-brent-friday-close-2026-10-24.png)

Friday's price was up 3.14% on the day, the highest in about six months, and it also ended the week behind your next petrol price. Every Wednesday the Ministry of Finance (MOF) resets the full price of RON95, RON97 and diesel, using the average price of the week before. Brent averaged about US$111 over this one, against about US$107 the week before.

On BUDI95, within 300 litres a month, you still pay RM1.99. The government covers RM2.33 of every BUDI95 litre this week, or 54% of the RM4.32 full price. A higher full price adds to that part one for one, and lands on full-price buyers and diesel businesses too. With shop prices 1.8% higher in September than a year earlier, fuel is the price most of us see move every week.

Friday's push came after Saudi Aramco, the Saudi state oil company, ordered staff out of its Dhahran headquarters as fighting with the Houthi group got worse. Andrew Lipow, an analyst at Lipow Oil Associates, said traders are also worried about continued Iranian attacks on tankers.

Still, analysts quoted by the news agency EFE see a risk premium in the price, extra money paid out of worry rather than for oil actually lost, so it could fall quickly if the fighting eases. MOF also weighs what ready-made petrol and diesel cost in the region, and those don't always follow crude. Wednesday evening, Oct 28, shows whether the more expensive week reaches the petrol pump.

## Also worth knowing

**Malaysia's leading index, an early signal for the economy, rose 2.2% in August from a year earlier.**

The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) builds this index to point to where the economy is heading over the next few months. On Friday it said August's reading grew faster than July's, and linked the outlook to strong buying of computer chips. For you, it's an early read on jobs and business activity in the months ahead.

**Petronas and two foreign partners will store carbon dioxide under the seabed off Peninsular Malaysia.**

Petronas said on Friday it signed a deal with France's TotalEnergies and Japan's Mitsui to set up a shared company, starting with an old offshore gas field that can take up to 5 million tonnes a year, from 2031 at the earliest. That's investment in Malaysia's energy sector, and a place for heavy industry here and nearby to send emissions.

**Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) has joined a Singapore-led project on moving digital money across borders.**

BLOOM works on how digital money, such as bank deposits in digital form, moves between countries. The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), Singapore's central bank, said on Friday that BNM and the Bank of Thailand joined. Maybank and ShopeePay are members too, so this is about pipes Malaysian banks and e-wallets could one day use abroad.

**US share prices fell again on Friday, and two main US indexes ended a second losing week.**

The Dow, a scoreboard of big US companies' share prices, dropped 1.23% as selling spread beyond AI shares and higher energy costs hit airlines. Two other scoreboards, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, ended the week lower. No clear line to Malaysia yet, but the drop shapes how investors feel before the US Federal Reserve decides next week.
