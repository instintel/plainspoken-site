*3-minute read*

The government will keep RON95 at RM1.99 a litre for BUDI95 drivers and is ready to carry the cost, even if it reaches RM42 billion to RM45 billion a year, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said in Melaka on Saturday. That's if oil prices keep climbing.

![Photo of petrol pumps at a fuel station.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-26/photo-fuel-subsidy-2026-10-26.jpg)

Budget 2027, presented on Oct 9, set aside about RM40 billion for the fuel subsidy, the money the government pays so you pay less at the petrol pump. So his figure runs up to about RM5 billion above what the Budget planned.

The fuel subsidy is already one of the biggest items in the Budget. It's why your own litre has stayed at RM1.99 while prices in the shops rose 1.8% in the year to September.

On a single litre, this week's full RON95 price is RM4.32. You pay RM1.99, so the government pays RM2.33 of every BUDI95 litre, or 54% of the full price. With your price fixed, every sen the full price goes up comes out of the government's share - one for one.

![How a rise in oil prices reaches the government's bill (Diagram). How oil prices reach the yearly fuel subsidy bill while BUDI95 holds RON95 at RM1.99 a litre. Prices for Oct 22 to 28.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-26/diagram-fuel-subsidy-2026-10-26.png)

Anwar said paying market prices instead would cost people an extra RM2 to RM5 a day, and about RM10 a day for a working family.

Meanwhile, the oil risk behind his "if" came back over the weekend, when Saudi Arabia and its allies said they shot down missiles and drones aimed at Saudi cities. No market traded, so oil prices didn't react.

On Wednesday, Oct 28, the Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets next week's prices, the first since he spoke. One week won't settle the yearly figure, though. If oil falls back over the coming weeks, the Budget's figure of about RM40 billion could still be enough. If it keeps climbing, nobody has said yet where the extra money would come from.

## Also from the weekend

**Anwar says one of the world's biggest investors plans to put US$50 billion into tech in Malaysia.**

He said an investor he didn't name told him last week that it had picked Malaysia for AI and data centres in Kuching in Sarawak, and in Peninsular Malaysia. That's about RM204 billion. He gave no timeline or job count, but said it could bring new and better jobs, especially for young people and graduates.

**The government launched a five-year plan to tackle the cost of living.**

The National Cost of Living Action Plan 2030 (NCLAP30) has 429 measures aimed at raising incomes and keeping basic prices stable. Datuk Armizan Mohd Ali, who heads the Ministry of Domestic Trade and Cost of Living (KPDN), said the goal is to carry out every measure well. It covers what households spend on most, from food and transport to housing and utilities.

**China says it firmly opposes a US plan to further limit US investment in its tech.**

China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) said on Saturday it reserves the right to respond with measures of its own. The US Treasury plan would add biotech, supercomputing and hypersonic systems to the fields it already limits. Malaysia implication remains unconfirmed.

**Indonesia started cutting the gas it pipes to Singapore, to keep more for itself.**

SKK Migas, Indonesia's oil and gas regulator, said the cuts began on Saturday. Indonesia already uses about 70% of its gas at home and is reviewing that split to keep more there. Malaysia implication remains unconfirmed.
