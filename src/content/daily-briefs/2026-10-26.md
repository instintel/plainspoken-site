*3-minute read*

On Oct 9, Budget 2027, the government's spending plan for next year, set aside about RM40 billion for fuel subsidies, the part of the petrol price the government pays for you. On Saturday in Melaka, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim put a bigger number on it. "If oil prices continue to surge, the cost borne by the government could reach between RM42 billion and RM45 billion a year," he said.

![Photo of petrol pumps at a fuel station.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-26/photo-fuel-subsidy-2026-10-26.jpg)

He also said RON95 stays at RM1.99 a litre on BUDI95, adding that paying the market price would cost people an extra RM2 to RM5 a day. So if you stay within your 300 litres a month, nothing changes at the petrol pump. The risk of a bigger bill sits with the government instead.

You can see that bill in a single litre. This week's full RON95 price, set by the Ministry of Finance (MOF), is RM4.32, so the government covers RM2.33 of every BUDI95 litre, or 54% of it. When the full price goes up, the government's part goes up by the same amount.

Whether oil keeps climbing is the part the government can't control. Over the weekend Saudi Arabia shot down more missiles and drones fired by the Houthis, an armed group in Yemen. US President Donald Trump also said he was still choosing between a deal with Iran and more strikes. Markets were shut, so no price has moved on it yet.

That leaves one open question behind Saturday's number. Keeping oil's swings off your tank could take up to about RM5 billion more than the Budget planned, and nobody has said yet where that money would come from.

## Also from the weekend

**A big global investor plans to put US$50 billion into AI and data centres in Malaysia, Anwar says.**

Anwar said the investor, which he did not name, told him about the plan on a video call last Tuesday. It picked Kuching in Sarawak and Peninsular Malaysia for the money, about RM204 billion. He gave no timeline, but said it could bring new and better jobs, especially for young people and graduates.

**The government launched a five-year plan with 429 measures on the cost of living.**

The plan covers eight areas households spend on most, including food, transport, housing and utilities, and aims to raise incomes and keep basic prices stable. Domestic Trade and Cost of Living Minister Armizan Mohd Ali said the goal is to make sure every measure is actually carried out. No single measure has been spelled out yet.

**China said it firmly opposes a US plan to widen limits on US investment in Chinese technology.**

The US rules already cover chips, quantum computing and AI, and the new proposal would add areas such as biotechnology. China's Ministry of Commerce called it discriminatory on Saturday and said it may hit back, without saying how. No clear line to Malaysia yet, but it's a fresh fight between the two over technology.

**Indonesia has started cutting the gas it pipes to Singapore.**

SKK Migas, Indonesia's upstream oil and gas regulator, said the cut began on Saturday. Indonesia already uses about 70% of its own gas at home and wants to keep more for its own industries. No clear line to Malaysia yet, since the pipelines in question run straight to Singapore.
