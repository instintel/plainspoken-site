*3-minute read*

On Friday, Brent crude, the global oil price everyone quotes, ended at US$116.72 a barrel, its highest in six months. On Monday it dropped to US$115.81, down 91 cents, or 0.8%.

![Photo of an oil tanker ship at sea.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-27/photo-brent-crude-2026-10-27.jpg)

That matters to you because oil decides how much the government spends keeping your petrol cheap. This week the full price of RON95 is RM4.32 a litre. BUDI95 drivers pay RM1.99, so the government pays RM2.33 on every litre. Budget 2027 set aside about RM40 billion for that fuel subsidy, and on Oct 24 Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said it could cost RM42 billion to RM45 billion a year if oil keeps climbing.

Oil fell, according to Reuters, on hopes of talking instead of fighting. Senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan, including Saudi Arabia's defence minister, met Iran's top security officials in Tehran, Iran's capital. It was the first high-level contact between Iran and the three since the fighting got worse. No deal came out of it, but the four agreed to keep talking.

Still, the drop was small, and the attacks haven't stopped. Yemen's Houthi rebels, who are attacking Saudi Arabia, said they hit pumping stations on a Saudi oil pipeline and Riyadh's airport. Saudi Arabia hasn't confirmed that. Reuters said those attacks are why oil didn't fall further.

So one day of 0.8% does little next to last week's 6% climb. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets next week's petrol price on Wednesday from the week before, so Monday's dip won't be in it. What would really move the subsidy bill is where the talks go from here: more talking, a deal that calms things down, or new fighting.

## Other things moving

**Singapore gave early approval to buy up to 1.3 gigawatts of green electricity from Vietnam and Malaysia.**

Singapore's energy regulator gave a Singapore power company and its partners, TNB and Petronas among them, the go-ahead on Monday, for power from 2035. It still needs approvals in each country, deals to sell the power and financing. If it goes ahead, TNB and Petronas would build solar and battery projects here, making electricity something we sell abroad.

**Getting out of bankruptcy should get faster next year, the law minister says.**

Azalina Othman Said told Parliament on Monday that the fast-track programme will cut the time it takes to get out of bankruptcy. Its Second Chance Policy has given 253,457 discharges since 2023, up to September. You apply online to the Malaysian Insolvency Department. Single parents, small-business borrowers and scam victims get priority, so they can get back to work and business sooner.

**Brazil's share market fell about 14% in one day after President Lula won a fourth term.**

Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, Brazil's left-wing president, won Sunday's runoff vote, a record fourth term. Investors sold hard on Monday, worried he could spend more and add to the country's debt.

**Anthropic, the US company behind the Claude AI chatbot, filed to list its shares in the US.**

It plans to list on Nasdaq, the US share market where many big tech firms trade, under the short name ANT. There's no date, size or price for the listing yet. US tech shares went up the same day, and the Nasdaq Composite, its main scoreboard, closed 0.82% higher.
