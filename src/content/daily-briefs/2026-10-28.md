*3-minute read*

If you're a Malaysian with a formal job and earn RM3,000 a month, you're right in the middle. That's the median wage. Line up all 7.2 million Malaysians in formal jobs from lowest pay to highest, and the person in the middle earned RM3,000 in June.

![Photo of Malaysian sen coins on a table.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-28/photo-median-wage-malaysia-2026-10-28.jpg)

The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) put that out on Tuesday. A year earlier, in June 2025, the middle pay was RM2,864, so it went up RM136 a month, or 4.8%. Prices rose too, just slower. Malaysia's inflation, how fast prices in the shops go up, was 1.9% in June. One number tracks the pay of Malaysians in formal jobs and the other tracks prices everyone pays, but for the worker in the middle, pay grew faster than the cost of things over that year.

![Malaysia's median wage has gone up every year since 2023, to RM3,000 (Chart). The median monthly pay of Malaysians in formal jobs, in ringgit, for June of each year. The median is the pay of the worker in the middle.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-28/chart-median-wage-malaysia-2026-10-28.png)

Prices are still rising slowly, by 1.8% in September. And in August, only 2.9% of people who wanted a job couldn't find one.

Fewer people are stuck at the bottom, too. In June, 8.2% of these workers earned below RM1,700, the minimum wage, down from 10.4% a year before. Still, the lowest-paid 10% earn RM1,700 or less, while the top 10% earn at least RM9,600.

And where you work changes the picture a lot:

- Kuala Lumpur: RM4,245, the highest middle pay of any state
- Kelantan: RM1,827, the lowest

DOSM gave no reason for the rise. Its next pay figures will show whether pay keeps running ahead of prices.

## Also on the radar

**The government is studying switching on the fuel-cost charge on electricity bills in stages from 2027.**

Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof told Parliament on Tuesday that the charge, the Automatic Fuel Adjustment (AFA), would protect users by category. He pointed to fuel prices tied to the West Asia conflict, and gas and coal costing more than expected. There's no date or rate yet, but it decides how much of those costs reaches your bill.

**There's no decision yet on government help for AirAsia, but two ministries are now reviewing proposals.**

Transport Minister Anthony Loke told Parliament on Tuesday that his ministry and the Ministry of Finance (MOF) are looking at ideas, after saying on Oct 12 that no talks had been held. Keeping flights running comes first, he said, warning a big airline failing would cost jobs. Any help would mean public money.

**Brent crude fell for a second day as talks to calm the Gulf picked up.**

Brent, the oil price everyone quotes, ended Tuesday at US$113.88 a barrel, down 1.7%. Bloomberg said traders were weighing moves to ease tension after senior Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials met Iran's government in Tehran. Oil feeds into MOF's weekly full RON95 price and what the government pays to keep BUDI95 at RM1.99.

**Profits at China's big industrial companies rose 14.4% in September from a year earlier, the fastest in three months.**

Figures from China's National Bureau of Statistics, its official statistics agency, show tech and electronics makers did far better than industry as a whole. China is Malaysia's biggest trading partner, so how its factories are doing bears on demand for what Malaysia sells.
