*3-minute read*

Malaysian producers got 12.0% more for the things they made in September than a year earlier. That figure is the Producer Price Index (PPI), the prices producers get, from the oil field to the farm, before anything reaches a shop, and the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) put it out on Wednesday.

![Photo of rows of steel drums with red and grey tops.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-29/photo-producer-prices-malaysia-2026-10-29.jpg)

Meanwhile, prices in the shops, the ones you pay, rose just 1.8% in September. The PPI also counts raw materials and goods sold abroad, while shop prices are what households pay, with some, like fuel, set or partly paid for by the government. So the two measure different baskets, and in September they were very far apart.

And producer prices are still speeding up. In February they were 3.4% lower than a year before. They've climbed every month since, to 10.7% in August, the figure we looked at on Sep 29, and now 12.0%.

![Producer prices in Malaysia have risen faster every month since February (Chart). How much the prices Malaysian producers get rose or fell compared with the same month a year earlier, in per cent, February to September 2026.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-29/chart-producer-prices-malaysia-2026-10-29.png)

DOSM says it's mostly oil. The price of crude petroleum coming out of the ground was 74.2% higher than a year earlier, and refined fuel products made here rose 35.0%. Farm prices rose just 2.9%.

If your business buys locally made fuel products or materials, these are the prices sitting behind your suppliers' bills. Where the rest of that extra cost lands, DOSM's figures don't say: with producers selling abroad, with businesses along the way, or later at the shops. October's producer and shop prices will show whether the gap starts to close. Until then, who's quietly holding the difference?

## Also happening

**The full price of RON95 goes up 10 sen to RM4.42 a litre from today.**

It's the first rise since last week's cut, and unsubsidised diesel goes up by even more. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) gave no reason, only that the government will keep protecting people and businesses from global uncertainty. So full-price petrol, and diesel for work vans, costs more until the next price on Wednesday, Nov 4.

**The US central bank kept its interest rate where it was, but two of its voters wanted a rise.**

Early this morning, Malaysian time, the Federal Reserve held at 3.75% to 4.00%, saying US inflation "remains elevated". Market pricing right after leaned towards a rise in December. A Bank Muamalat economist says the ringgit stays sensitive to US interest rates, which matters for anything you pay or borrow in US dollars.

**Customs at KLIA stopped AI-chip servers worth nearly RM49.5 million passing through Malaysia without a permit.**

KLIA Customs said a syndicate used Malaysia as a stopover so the servers would face no export limits at their next stop, and the chips could help build military technology. It's the fifth case this year from the same group. Chips like these need a permit from the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) before they land here.

**Canada's central bank raised its interest rate to 2.5%, its first rise in more than three years.**

The Bank of Canada said it acted to limit the risk of high energy prices, linked to the Middle East conflict, spreading into the prices of everything else. It now expects inflation to stay above its target until mid-2028, and its governor, Tiff Macklem, said it "could need to increase the policy rate further".
