*3-minute read*

US prices rose 3.6% in the year to September, the last of three things out of the US in about a day:

- Early Thursday, Malaysian time: the Federal Reserve, the US central bank, kept its interest rate at 3.75% to 4.00%, with two of its voters wanting a rise.
- Thursday, 6pm: the ringgit closed stronger at 4.0575 to the US dollar, from 4.0745 on Wednesday.
- Thursday, 8:30pm: that price figure, on the measure the Fed uses for its 2% goal. In August it was 3.4%.

![Photo of a small gold shopping trolley on a pavement.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-30/photo-us-inflation-2026-10-30.jpg)

That measure is the PCE price index. So US prices are well above where the Fed wants them, and moving further away. Here at home, shop prices rose just 1.8% in September, and Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) has kept the OPR, the main interest rate behind most loans here, at 2.75% since July 2025.

Meanwhile the US economy is speeding up. US figures show it grew at a 3.4% yearly pace from July to September, from 2.2% in the three months before. And the US buys a lot from us. Malaysian companies sold RM282.07 billion of goods there from January to September, up 69.1%.

Rising prices plus strong growth keep another Fed rise in play. On Thursday, traders' bets put the chance of a rise on Dec 10, our time, at 79.6%. Bank Muamalat's chief economist, Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid, said the Fed could start raising again in December as inflation stays high, though it's not certain. That matters for the ringgit and anything you pay in US dollars.

BNM goes first, next Thursday, Nov 5. If October's US price figures slow down, the case for a December rise gets weaker.

## Other things moving

**On average, each worker in Malaysia added RM104,556 worth of value to the economy in 2025.**

That's from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM)'s yearly productivity report, out on Thursday, and the figure grew faster in 2025 than it did in 2024. Construction workers saw the biggest jump. The gap between states is still wide, though: Sabah has the lowest value per worker, while Johor improved the fastest.

**The government is confident Malaysia's economy will grow more than 5% this year.**

Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah Azizan told Parliament on Thursday, wrapping up the Ministry of Finance (MOF)'s part of the Budget 2027 debate. He pointed to DOSM's figures for the first nine months, and said it would be a third straight year above that level. DOSM's final July to September figure is out on Nov 18.

**Lynas will shut its rare earths processing plant near Kuantan for about a month for maintenance.**

Lynas, the Australian company that runs it, said the work is electrical checks and kiln maintenance it does every three years, before the end of December. So the plant will make less this quarter than from July to September. No dates were given, but acting chief executive Pol Le Roux said stockpiles will keep customer orders filled.

**Shares of Alphabet, Google's parent company, fell 12.6% on Thursday, their worst day since they started trading more than 20 years ago.**

Alphabet's latest quarterly results missed what analysts expected for both sales and profit. The fall dragged US share prices lower. Microsoft, which beat forecasts, rose before the market opened.

## What to watch

- **Thursday November 5:** BNM decides on the OPR, Malaysia's main interest rate behind most home and car loans, and whether it stays at 2.75%.
- **Wednesday November 18:** DOSM's final figure for how fast Malaysia's economy grew from July to September.
- **Thursday December 10:** the Fed's next rate decision, Malaysian time, on whether the US raises its main interest rate again, which can move the ringgit.
