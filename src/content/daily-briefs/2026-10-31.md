*3-minute read*

Friday afternoon, Bursa Malaysia's main KLCI index closed up 0.28%. That night in the US, stocks had their worst day since June. The Nasdaq, a US share index full of tech companies, fell 2.86%, and Intel, the US chipmaker, saw its shares drop about 29%, its worst day since 1974.

![Photo of a computer chip on a circuit board.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-31/photo-tech-stocks-2026-10-31.jpg)

Intel isn't a faraway name for Malaysia. It has been assembling and testing chips in Penang since 1972, and in 2023 it committed RM30 billion over 10 years to grow its Penang and Kulim operations. Electronics, the chips and parts our factories make, were more than half of Malaysia's exports in September.

Intel's fall came after its own forecast. It expects October to December sales of US$15 billion to US$16 billion, below the US$17.07 billion analysts expected. It also warned PC demand could stay weak until at least the first half of 2027, as higher memory chip prices push up what PCs cost. Amazon fell too after missing sales and profit forecasts. AP said investors are worried all that AI spending may not turn into profits soon enough, and one fund manager called this the "show me" phase of the AI trade.

Asia felt the same worry earlier in the day. Shares fell 3.48% in South Korea and 2.12% in Japan on concerns about AI investment. On Bursa, the KLCI rose, led by banks, but chip-linked companies such as Malaysian Pacific Industries were among the biggest losers.

![Malaysia's KLCI rose on Friday while Korea, Taiwan and Japan fell (Chart). How each market's main share index changed on Friday, Oct 30, in per cent. These markets closed before the US sell-off that night.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-10-31/chart-asia-share-indexes-2026-10-31.png)

The Nasdaq ended October with its worst month since March 2025. Bursa trades again on Monday. If chip stocks bounce back next week, Friday reads as one bad day. If they don't, how much longer will investors wait for AI spending to pay?

## Elsewhere on Friday

**Brent crude ended its December contract at US$117.64, its highest close since the end of April.**

Reuters said prices rose as a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, a waterway oil tankers use, was hit by explosives from a small boat. From Monday, the quoted price is January's contract, which ended lower. Oil feeds into the full fuel price the Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets for next week on Wednesday.

**Two companies plan an industrial and business hub in Johor's special zone with Singapore, worth an estimated RM4 billion.**

UEM Sunrise and AME Elite said on Friday they'll jointly develop land in Gerbang Nusajaya inside the Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ). The RM4 billion is what the two companies estimate the finished project will be worth. Work on the first phase is due to start by end-2027.

**The ringgit ended Friday a little stronger at 4.0555 to the US dollar, after touching its strongest level in nearly two months.**

It was 4.0575 on Thursday. Bank Muamalat's chief economist said traders are rethinking how likely a US rate rise is, and that pulled the US dollar down. A stronger ringgit makes anything priced in US dollars a little cheaper for you. Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) decides on its own rate on Thursday.

**Japan's central bank kept its interest rate at 1.25%, and said price rises are now broadly in line with its goal.**

It's the first time the Bank of Japan (BOJ) has judged underlying price rises that way since it set its target in 2013. One board member voted for a rise. Most economists in a Reuters poll expect a rise this quarter, most of them at the BOJ's Dec 17 to 18 meeting.
