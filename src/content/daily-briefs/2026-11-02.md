*3-minute read*

China's factories had a worse October than September, and the official survey that tracks them fell back under the line between growing and shrinking. Its score came out on Saturday at 49.3, down from 50.1. That's the third time this year it has dropped below the line straight after a month above it. China matters here because it's Malaysia's biggest trading partner country, with 18.3% of all the trade Malaysia did with the world in September.

![Photo of a factory production line with machines.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-02/photo-china-factory-activity-2026-11-02.jpg)

The score is called the purchasing managers' index (PMI). Every month, China's official statistics agency, the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), asks factory managers whether business got better or worse than the month before. Above 50, more said better. Below 50, more said worse.

![China's factory survey fell back below 50 in October (Chart). China's official monthly survey of factory managers, in index points, October 2025 to October 2026. The line at 50 marks no change from the month before.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-02/chart-china-factory-pmi-2026-11-02.png)

Inside the survey, the drop was wide. Chinese factories made less and got fewer new orders, and the bureau's matching score for services and construction firms fell under 50 too. The bureau put it down partly to customers buying early, before the National Day holiday, and partly to a complex world outside China.

All year, the score has moved back and forth around 50. Five of the 10 months so far in 2026 came in below it.

Malaysia's own factory survey for October, the first Malaysian read, is out this morning at 8:30am. China's November score will then test the bureau's holiday explanation.

## Also this weekend

**Malaysia's and Singapore's prime ministers could witness the Johor zone's master plan launch on Dec 4.**

Economy Minister Akmal Nasrullah Mohd Nasir said on Saturday that Anwar Ibrahim and Lawrence Wong had agreed, and that the launch could coincide with their yearly retreat. The plan is for the Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ), where Johor hopes to pull in new investment and jobs. Ministers from both sides meet in Singapore today to check progress.

**Malaysia Airlines says customer details from the past three years may be affected by a breach at its booking-system company.**

The airline said on Saturday that information handled by Amadeus, the outside company running its booking system, between August 2023 and September 2026 may be affected. Its own systems weren't hit, and there's no sign so far that financial information was accessed. It's telling customers to watch for messages asking for personal or financial details.

**Big oil producers kept their December output limits unchanged on Sunday, though most still pump well below them.**

The core members of OPEC+, a group of big oil-producing countries including Saudi Arabia and Russia, made the call, as expected, with exports disrupted by the Iran war. The full group is expected to set 2027 limits on Nov 29. The oil price feeds the full RON95 price and what the government pays to subsidise each litre.

**The FBI says hackers linked to North Korea were behind the theft of about US$6.3 billion in crypto from Binance, the biggest on record.**

Binance, the crypto exchange, lost the coins on or about Oct 29. It says it has already replaced most of them with its own money and will pay customers back in full. The FBI expects the hackers to try moving the coins through other crypto services.
