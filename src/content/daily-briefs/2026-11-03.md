*3-minute read*

Malaysia's factories had their best month since April, with their monthly survey score rising to 51.0 in October from 49.9 in September, and they hired more people for a third month in a row. Above 50 means more factory bosses said business got better than said it got worse, and April's score was 51.6.

![Photo of a printed circuit board, close up.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-03/photo-malaysia-pmi-2026-11-03.jpg)

The score is called the [purchasing managers' index (PMI)](/explainers/pmi/). Every month S&P Global, the firm that runs the survey, asks about 400 Malaysian manufacturers how things compare with the month before. Meanwhile, Malaysia's exports in September were 54.5% higher than a year earlier.

![Malaysia's factory survey rose back above 50 in October (Chart). S&P Global's monthly survey of about 400 Malaysian factories, in index points, October 2025 to October 2026. Above the line at 50, more firms said business improved.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-03/chart-malaysia-factory-pmi-2026-11-03.png)

The better month came from orders. Factories got new orders at one of the fastest rates in four and a half years, mostly from customers they already had. S&P Global economist Maryam Baluch said orders came back "supported in part by stronger demand for AI-related products".

Still, the same survey shows the bill going up. Factories paid more for transport and materials, which they partly put down to the Middle East conflict, and raised their own selling prices at the same faster pace. Those are factory prices, not shop prices. Factory bosses were also the least confident they've been since April.

This year the score has crossed 50 both ways, with February, May and September all below it. S&P Global says October points to growth carrying on this quarter. The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) puts out its full growth figure for July to September on Nov 18, and November's PMI comes on Dec 1. Those will show whether October was a one-off or the start of a steadier pickup.

## Other things moving

**A Malaysian bank economist expects Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) to raise its main interest rate on Thursday, while most expect no change.**

Bank Muamalat Malaysia's chief economist, Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid, says fears of slower growth after the tariff shock didn't come true. HSBC economists see a rise only early next year. That rate, [the OPR](/explainers/opr/), now 2.75%, is what banks use to price floating-rate home, car and personal loans.

**About RM40.3 billion of the investment approved for the Johor-Singapore zone has turned into real projects.**

That's a bit under half of what was approved from January 2025 to June 2026, says a joint statement from both governments after Malaysian and Singapore ministers met in Singapore on Monday. The projects already come with thousands of jobs in Johor, and the zone's master plan is due when the two leaders meet in December.

**Oil prices dropped sharply on Monday after Iran's leaders gave the go-ahead for peace talks.**

[Brent](/explainers/brent-oil-price/), the global oil price most often quoted, ended at US$104.77 a barrel, from US$113.43 on Friday. Iran's president said the supreme leader had allowed the government to talk, and Trump said "no" to resuming strikes after Tuesday's US vote. There's no deal yet. The oil price feeds the full RON95 price and the fuel subsidy bill.

**US factories grew faster in October, with output at its quickest pace since 2021.**

The main US factory survey, from the Institute for Supply Management, rose to 55.5 from 54.5, its tenth month of growth in a row. US factories said tariffs, and petroleum-based products made dearer by the Middle East conflict, kept pushing their costs up.
