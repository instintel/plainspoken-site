*3-minute read*

The [ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) got a little weaker on Tuesday as investors across Asia pulled money out of AI bets, and one fund manager said it got caught up because Malaysia has gained from the AI boom. At the 6pm close, one US dollar cost RM4.0735, up from RM4.0695 on Monday, so anything you pay for in US dollars cost slightly more ringgit.

![Photo of a green computer circuit board, close up.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-04/photo-ringgit-2026-11-04.jpg)

It started in South Korea, where the main share index, the Kospi, fell 3.59% as foreign investors sold about US$1.13 billion of Korean shares. Korean media put it down to doubts that tech companies' huge AI spending will pay off.

Stephen Innes, a fund manager at SPI Asset Management, said "the ringgit is getting caught up in the broader unwinding of the AI trade". Malaysia has gained from AI investment through its chip industry, he said, so when investors back away from AI, the ringgit feels it. Chips and electronics made up about half of what Malaysia sold abroad in September.

It was a small move, about 0.4 sen. One US dollar cost RM4.0865 on Oct 27 and RM4.0555 on Oct 30, so the ringgit is still stronger than a week ago.

![The ringgit has given back some of last week's gains (Chart). Ringgit needed for one US dollar at the 6pm close each trading day, Oct 19 to Nov 3, 2026. A lower number means a stronger ringgit.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-04/chart-ringgit-us-dollar-2026-11-04.png)

Back home, banks kept Bursa Malaysia's [KLCI](/explainers/klci/), the index of the biggest listed companies, up 0.14%, though more shares fell than rose.

Then US chip shares went the other way. Once Asia closed, they bounced, and later chipmaker AMD said sales of its data centre chips nearly doubled from a year earlier.

So Wednesday's trading in Asia will show whether Tuesday was a one-day pullback. Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) decides on its main interest rate on Thursday, and US jobs numbers come out on Friday.

## Elsewhere on Tuesday

**Australia's central bank raised its main interest rate to 4.85%, its highest since 2008.**

The Reserve Bank of Australia said prices there are still rising faster than it wants, with higher fuel costs, partly from the Middle East conflict, feeding into other prices. It expects inflation to stay above its target until late 2027. It was the bank's fifth rise this year, even as it said Australia's economy is slowing and house prices have fallen.

**Malaysia's tax collector brought in 5.8% more direct tax in the first ten months of this year.**

From January to October, the Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (IRB) collected RM174.48 billion, against RM164.92 billion a year earlier, the Prime Minister said in a speech read out on Tuesday. He partly credited more people and companies paying the tax they owe. That money helps pay for the Budget, which MPs are debating until Nov 26.

**The global oil price fell for a second day, despite a reported US strike on an Iranian island.**

[Brent](/explainers/brent-oil-price/), the price most often quoted, ended Tuesday at US$103.60 a barrel, from US$104.77. A US newspaper reported a strike on Iran's Qeshm Island, but prices fell as go-between Qatar said talks had progressed. A senior Iranian official says a deal is still far off. That price feeds the full RON95 price and the fuel subsidy bill.

**So far this year, the government has paid out RM20.27 billion in income tax refunds.**

Individual taxpayers and small businesses get their money back first, Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah told the Dewan Rakyat on Tuesday. He said the number of taxpayers still waiting for tax to be refunded has fallen by almost half since the end of 2025, and that the rest will be paid promptly.
