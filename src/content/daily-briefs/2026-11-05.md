*3-minute read*

Google is putting another US$15 billion, about RM61 billion, into [data centres](/explainers/data-centres/) in Malaysia in 2027 and 2028, and it expects the building work alone to support more than 37,000 local jobs a year. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim was at the announcement on Wednesday.

![Photo of server racks with green and blue lights in a dark data centre.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-05/photo-google-malaysia-2026-11-05.jpg)

A data centre is a huge building full of computers that run cloud storage and AI apps. Google will grow its first Malaysian campus at Elmina in Selangor and build new ones in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan, on top of the US$2 billion it announced in 2024. So if you work in construction, engineering or tech, that's where Google expects the jobs.

Once the campuses are running, Google projects more than 9,000 jobs, paying on average 45% above the national median, the middle of Malaysian pay. Fewer jobs than the build, but they last.

Power and water are the big worry with data centres, since they use a lot of electricity and often water for cooling. In July the deputy prime minister told Parliament they could use a third of Malaysia's energy by 2035, AFP reported. Google says it will add more than one gigawatt of clean power to the national grid and pay for all the power its campuses use. It will also build a Negeri Sembilan water plant that goes to the state. On the environment, Anwar said the government had set conditions, and Google accepted them.

Meanwhile, Malaysia's economy grew 5.3% in July to September, by the government statisticians' first estimate.

That same night in the US, data centre shares fell after Democrats won Congress, on worries AI building there could slow. For Malaysia, the test comes when construction starts in 2027.

## Other things worth knowing

**The ringgit got stronger after Democrats won control of the US Congress.**

One US dollar cost RM4.0610 at Wednesday's 6pm close, from RM4.0735 a day earlier, so US-dollar prices got slightly cheaper for you. Fund manager Stephen Innes said a Democratic Congress makes more US government spending less likely, which could help Asian currencies a little. Bigger gains need traders to scale back bets on a US interest rate rise in December, he said.

**Full-price RON95 petrol drops 15 sen this week.**

From today to Nov 11, full-price [RON95](/explainers/ron95-price/) costs RM4.27 a litre, down from RM4.42, with diesel and RON97 cheaper too. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) put it down to more refined fuel in the region, including Singapore, and oil prices falling at the end of the week its formula uses. The government also pays a little less on each BUDI95 litre.

**The global oil price fell below US$95 after the US election result.**

[Brent](/explainers/brent-oil-price/), the most quoted oil price, ended Wednesday at US$94.62 a barrel, from US$103.60. Traders bet the new Congress would help end the war with Iran sooner, Reuters reported, and Trump said the US has no immediate plans to strike Iran. An Iranian official says a deal is still far off. That price feeds the full RON95 price and the fuel subsidy bill.

**STR cash aid worth RM1.1 billion goes out this week, ahead of Deepavali.**

It goes to millions of lower-income households and single seniors from Wednesday to Friday, MOF said, and Anwar said it helps with rising living costs. If you're newly approved or won an appeal, your payment comes from Nov 16.
