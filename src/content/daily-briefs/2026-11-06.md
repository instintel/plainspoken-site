*3-minute read*

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) kept [the OPR](/explainers/opr/) at 2.75% on Thursday, its last decision of the year, even though one bank economist had expected a rise. The OPR is the main interest rate BNM sets, and banks build floating-rate loans on it, so if your loan is the floating kind, your monthly instalment stays where it is for now.

![Photo of a black pocket calculator on a white table.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-06/photo-opr-2026-11-06.jpg)

Most economists saw the hold coming. Fifteen of 21 in a Bloomberg poll expected it, and the rate hasn't moved since BNM cut it from 3.00% in July 2025. Bank Muamalat's chief economist, Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid, had expected a rise that morning.

![BNM has kept the OPR at 2.75% since July 2025 (Chart). Bank Negara Malaysia's Overnight Policy Rate after each meeting since January 2023, in per cent. The dots mark the last rise and the cut. Marked: May 3, 2023, OPR raised to 3.00%; Jul 9, 2025, OPR cut to 2.75%.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-06/chart-opr-2026-11-06.png)

Meanwhile, BNM said prices in Malaysia rose 1.8% on average so far this year, the same pace as September's official reading. It expects that to edge higher in 2027 because of tensions abroad, high prices for raw materials and bad weather, while staying under control. It also expects the economy to grow more than 5% this year.

So the argument has moved to 2027. Economists at OCBC expect a rise early in the year, and RHB sees the OPR at 3.00% within the first half. Others see no rush, with fund manager Stephen Innes saying BNM is "keeping their options open rather than putting a rate hike on the calendar."

On the day, [the ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) got a little stronger, closing at 4.0445 to the US dollar from 4.0610, so US-dollar prices cost you slightly less. Innes says the ringgit is taking its cues from events abroad, mainly oil prices and the US central bank, rather than from BNM.

October's inflation figure, out on Thursday, Nov 19, is the first test of whether prices have started to edge up.

## Elsewhere on Thursday

**Japanese retailer Daiso opened its biggest warehouse in the world at Port Klang.**

The RM1.2 billion centre at Pulau Indah will send goods from suppliers in Japan, China and elsewhere to 27 countries and territories. Malaysian firms help run it, with PKT Logistics signed up for warehouse work and freight. Transport Minister Anthony Loke also said Westports' container capacity should roughly double under its expansion, though he gave no date.

**US tech shares fell 5.5% in a day as investors dumped AI and data centre stocks.**

That was the drop in the Nasdaq, the main US index of tech shares. Investors worried the Democrats' wins in this week's US elections could bring limits on new data centres and closer checks on AI spending. The next test is the US jobs report on Friday night.

**The UK's central bank raised interest rates for the first time in more than three years.**

The Bank of England took its rate to 4% on Thursday, from 3.75%, saying fuel costs that jumped after the Iran war have pushed up UK prices. Governor Andrew Bailey said it may not be the last rise unless the Middle East conflict is settled.

**Oil bounced back after more tankers were reported hit in the Gulf.**

[Brent](/explainers/brent-oil-price/), the global oil price most often quoted, ended at US$98.29 a barrel, up from US$94.62 a day earlier. At least five ships have reported incidents since Sunday, and Iran said two tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported. This week's oil prices feed the full fuel prices the Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets next Wednesday.
