*3-minute read*

[The KLCI](/explainers/klci/), Bursa Malaysia's scoreboard of 30 big companies, fell 1.17% on Friday to 1,616.58, wiping out Thursday's gain almost to the point, as the selling in AI shares that started in the US reached Asia. The index had risen 19.30 points on Thursday. On Friday it lost 19.28.

![Close-up photo of the surface of a computer chip, in gold and green.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-07/photo-klci-2026-11-07.jpg)

Bursa's tech companies were hit hardest. Their index fell more than 6% soon after the open, with Malaysian Pacific Industries (MPI) among the biggest fallers. Investors worried that the Democrats' wins in the US midterm elections could bring tighter rules on AI, The Edge reported. [The ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) weakened too, to 4.0565 per US dollar from 4.0445, so anything priced in US dollars costs you a little more. Fund manager Stephen Innes said investors turned to the US dollar for safety, and that spilled over into Asian currencies.

Bursa's fall was far smaller than in Tokyo and Seoul, where shares fell about 6% each.

Rakuten Trade's Thong Pak Leng said the weakness came mostly from uncertainty abroad rather than a weakening Malaysian economy, while Pheim Asset Management's Khoo Zing Sheng said it's too early to tell whether AI growth is really slowing or prices are just correcting. At home, the Department of Statistics Malaysia's (DOSM) first estimate, out in October, had the economy growing 5.3% in July to September.

Later that night, after Bursa had shut, the Nasdaq, the main US index of tech shares, rose 1.1%. Bursa is closed on Monday for Deepavali, so Tuesday is the first look at whether Malaysian tech shares follow that rebound.

## Other things that moved

**The Employees Provident Fund (EPF) is one of three buyers offering RM4.03 billion for the rest of Yinson.**

The EPF, which holds Malaysians' retirement savings, teamed up with MISC, which runs an offshore energy business, and Yinson's founding Lim family. Yinson runs floating vessels that produce and store oil and gas at sea. Its board considers the offer next, and the company leaves Bursa only if approvals come through.

**US employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far more than economists expected.**

Economists had forecast about 60,000. The US unemployment rate went down too, and traders raised their bets that [the Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/), the US central bank, lifts rates in December. That matters here because, as Innes said this week, expected US rate rises limit how far the ringgit can strengthen. US inflation figures come out on Tuesday.

**Palm oil had its worst week in almost two years.**

Its main contract fell 9% over the week. Traders pointed to cheaper rival oils like soybean oil, record stockpiles in Malaysia and weaker crude oil, which makes palm less attractive for biodiesel. Palm oil is one of Malaysia's big exports, so its price shapes what planters and smallholders get paid.

**Global oil prices had their biggest weekly fall since 2022.**

Brent, the oil price most often quoted, fell about 13% as traders saw signs of progress towards ending the Iran war and Gulf producers restored more shipments. Saudi Arabia cut its prices for Asian buyers to a six-year low, Bloomberg reported. There's no deal yet. This week's prices feed the full fuel prices the Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets on Wednesday.
