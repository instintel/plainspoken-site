*3-minute read*

At 7am on Saturday, the first East Coast Rail Link (ECRL) trial train left Gombak with 170 passengers and pulled into Kuantan 88 minutes later. Paying passengers get their turn from Dec 12, with Economy seats all the way to Kota Bharu starting at RM125.

![Photo of a passenger train curving through green, forested hills.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-09/photo-ecrl-2026-11-09.jpg)

Saturday was also handover day. The contractor that built the first phase passed the 523 km line from Kota Bharu to Gombak to Malaysia Rail Link (MRL), the company that owns the project. MRL says it's fully ready to run. Bernama described it as finished ahead of schedule. The line has 16 stations, and the trial train, which hit up to 160 km/h on the way to Kuantan, went on to reach Kota Bharu at 10:58am.

From now until Dec 2, trial trains run on the same timetable paying passengers will get, in both directions.

MRL's chief executive, Darwis Abdul Razak, expects the railway to bring investment, tourists, jobs, new hubs for storing and moving goods, and industrial parks to the East Coast states. He gave no figure for it. The railway opens into an economy that [grew 5.3% in July to September](/explainers/gdp/) compared with a year earlier, by the Department of Statistics Malaysia's (DOSM) first estimate out in October.

This is still only the first phase. Phase 2, from Gombak to Port Klang, is on track, MRL says, with no date given. The next real step is mid-November, when ticket sales details are due through ECRL's official channels.

## Also over the weekend

**Malaysia's second 5G network reached 90% coverage in October, nine months ahead of schedule.**

5G is the faster kind of mobile internet your phone can use. Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil gave the figure at a telecoms conference in Doha on Sunday, and said service quality, affordability and resilience matter alongside coverage.

**A tribunal ruled that GrabCar's Auto Accept broke the Gig Workers Act, and awarded one driver RM2,000.**

Auto Accept took bookings before drivers knew the type of job or what they'd earn, the Human Resources Ministry (Kesuma) said on Saturday. If you drive for Grab, the Gig Workers Tribunal recommended you be told both before accepting. Kesuma says it will step up enforcement of the Act through the tribunal.

**Bank Rakyat says customers' money and personal data were not affected when its website went down.**

The bank blamed a technical problem at an outside service provider and said only its corporate website was hit, with banking running normally. Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said the bank reported the site was hacked, limited to changing what its pages showed, and that attacks so far had stolen no data.

**A missile attack on Sunday damaged a building owned by Saudi Aramco in Riyadh's financial district.**

Aramco is Saudi Arabia's oil giant. Witnesses saw several damaged floors and shattered glass. Saudi Arabia and its allies said their air defences destroyed two missiles, which they blamed on the Houthis, the group they're fighting in Yemen. No one was reported hurt, and no damage to oil supply was reported from this attack.
