*3-minute read*

SoftBank Group, Grab and Sarawak's state energy company PETROS signed agreements on Monday putting US$1.85 billion of seed money into AI infrastructure in Malaysia, the first figure the companies have put their own names to. On Oct 8 they had only agreed to explore the idea. On Oct 24, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said SoftBank's boss Masayoshi Son had picked Malaysia for US$50 billion of investment.

![Photo of colourful network cable plugs standing in a row against a white background.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-10/photo-softbank-malaysia-2026-11-10.jpg)

Seed money is the first cash that gets a project moving. This lot goes into two projects, one in Sarawak and one in Peninsular Malaysia, aiming long term for 5 gigawatts or more of AI computing capacity, the computer power AI tools run on. For you, the pitch is jobs: Anwar expects skilled jobs for Malaysians and work for local suppliers, though he gave no figure. The Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA), the agency that approves investment projects, counted RM218.5 billion of approved investment in Malaysia from January to June.

Here's who is putting in what:

- SoftBank Group, the Tokyo-listed tech investor: US$1 billion, more than half, and it leads the Sarawak project
- Grab, the company behind the app: US$750 million, and it leads the Peninsular one
- PETROS, Sarawak's state-owned energy company: US$100 million

![SoftBank puts in more than half of the first US$1.85 billion (Chart). The seed money each company puts into AI infrastructure in Malaysia, in US$ million, under agreements signed on Nov 9, 2026. It is paid in stages.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-10/chart-softbank-malaysia-seed-capital-2026-11-10.png)

The money comes in stages, paid out as the projects hit milestones and sign up customers, and final agreements and government approvals are still to come. Anwar said the projects must follow federal and state rules, including on renewable energy.

SoftBank hasn't confirmed the PM's US$50 billion, and the companies' announcements on Monday don't mention it. So look out next for the final agreements and approvals, and a first big customer, since the money is tied to those steps.

**Eligible flood-hit families in Selangor get an extra RM2,500 each, starting today.**

Anwar announced the cash on Monday, on top of the RM1,000 the National Disaster Management Agency (Nadma) already pays, which he said is often not enough for the heavier damage. It's meant for families who were displaced for a long time or whose homes were badly damaged, to replace household things. The agency running it will announce who qualifies and how to claim.

**Malaysia's cyber agency counted 5,110 cyber incidents in the first nine months of 2026.**

That's the National Cyber Security Agency (Nacsa). Its chief said a viral online map ranking Malaysia among the most-attacked countries counts attempts spotted by certain security products, and an attempt doesn't always mean a break-in. Nacsa rates Malaysia's threat level "moderate". It says people get targeted through hacked social media, email and online banking accounts.
