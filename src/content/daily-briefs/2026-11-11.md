*3-minute read*

Taiwan has put its investment talks with Malaysia on hold, its foreign ministry confirmed on Tuesday, saying it wants to protect Taiwan's sovereignty, its economic security and Taiwanese companies investing here. Those companies matter for jobs in Malaysia, with Taiwan's office in KL saying in February that Taiwanese investment supports about 500,000 local jobs.

![Photo of a green circuit board, seen up close.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-11/photo-taiwan-malaysia-investment-2026-11-11.jpg)

Last year, Taiwan was Malaysia's fourth-largest trading partner, and by its office's count it's the seventh-largest investor here. For scale, the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA), the agency that approves investment projects, counted RM218.5 billion of approved investment in Malaysia from January to June.

The row goes back to August. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said in a TV interview with Al Jazeera that he saw Taiwan as "part of China, period", and Taiwan's foreign ministry condemned it at the time. Anwar later said Malaysia's long-held position of recognising one China dates back to Tun Abdul Razak's government, and that any solution should be peaceful.

The pause started last month, reports say, though none gives an exact date. Industry voices quoted in a Nikkei Asia report on Monday worry about what the political uncertainty could mean for chip investment, after a run of Taiwanese tech expansion here.

For now, Taiwan says it's still talking to Malaysia and hopes the negotiations restart, and Malaysia hadn't responded publicly by Wednesday morning. So the next thing to watch is a restart date, or the next Taiwanese chip company deciding where its new plant goes.

## Elsewhere today

**US prices rose faster than expected in October, lifting bets on a bigger US rate rise.**

[US consumer prices](/explainers/us-inflation/) went up 0.4% in October from September, against the 0.3% economists polled by Reuters expected. There's no yearly figure, because no prices were collected in October 2025 during a lapse in US government funding. Prices outside food and energy landed where Fed Governor Christopher Waller, a US rate-setter, said he'd consider a bigger rise in December.

**Malaysia's factories, mines and power plants produced 6.3% more in September than a year earlier, beating forecasts.**

That's the Department of Statistics Malaysia's (DOSM) industrial production index. Factories led the way, especially electronics, while mines produced less. RHB's research team expects factories to stay busy on the global electronics upswing. The figure feeds into [Malaysia's growth number](/explainers/gdp/) for July to September, which DOSM releases on Nov 18.

**Malaysia's palm oil stockpile rose to 3.62 million tonnes in October, the highest on record, Reuters says.**

The Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB), which publishes the monthly figures, counted more oil coming out of plantations and more going out as exports, and the pile grew by less than traders expected. Palm oil is one of Malaysia's big exports, earning money for planters and smallholders.

**China's trade surplus passed US$1 trillion in the first 10 months of 2026.**

A trade surplus is how much more a country sells abroad than it buys. Reuters says the global race to build AI infrastructure boosted China's high-tech exports. The figure from China's customs agency lands a week before US President Donald Trump and China's Xi Jinping meet at the APEC summit of Asia-Pacific leaders in Shenzhen, China.
