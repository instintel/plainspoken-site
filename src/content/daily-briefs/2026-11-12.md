*3-minute read*

Bank shares pulled Malaysia's main share index below 1,600 on Wednesday, even though more shares on Bursa Malaysia rose than fell. The [ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) weakened against the US dollar the same day, so anything you pay for in US dollars, from travel to imported goods, costs a bit more in ringgit.

![Photo of a price chart on a computer screen, seen up close and out of focus.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-12/photo-klci-2026-11-12.jpg)

The [FBM KLCI](/explainers/klci/), a scoreboard of 30 of the biggest companies listed here, closed down 0.55% at 1,595.19, as Maybank, Public Bank and Hong Leong Bank dropped. Across Bursa, 686 shares rose and 427 fell. The ringgit ended at 4.0710 to the US dollar at 6pm, from 4.0540 on Tuesday, though it actually went up against the euro, the pound, the yen and the Thai baht.

![The ringgit weakened to 4.0710 per US dollar on Wednesday (Chart). Ringgit per US dollar at Bernama's 6pm close, each Bursa trading day from Oct 19 to Nov 11, 2026. A higher number means a weaker ringgit.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-12/chart-ringgit-us-dollar-2026-11-12.png)

Analysts trace both moves back to Tuesday night. US prices rose 0.4% in October, faster than the 0.3% forecast, and investment strategist Mohd Sedek Jantan says that strengthened bets on the [US Federal Reserve (the Fed)](/explainers/federal-reserve/) keeping interest rates higher for longer.

Rakuten Trade's Thong Pak Leng also pointed to US bond yields, the interest the US government pays to borrow, which were near 5%. Mohd Sedek warned that higher US yields and a stronger US dollar could keep pressure on Malaysian shares and the ringgit, and hold back foreign money flowing into Malaysia's markets. If the banks and the ringgit win back Wednesday's losses with no fresh US news, it was a one-day reaction.

For now, the Fed's main rate sits at 3.75% to 4.00% after it held in October. Bank Muamalat's chief economist, Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid, says the odds of a rise at its next meeting are now above 80%, and that decision lands on Thursday, Dec 10, Malaysia time.

## Other things moving

**The World Bank wants Malaysia to let small businesses use machines and unpaid invoices as security for loans.**

In a report launched on Wednesday, it says Malaysian small businesses pay for most of what they invest in, like machines, themselves. Its fix is letting them pledge machinery, stock or money customers owe them, not only property. Economy Minister Akmal Nasrullah Mohd Nasir said "The real issue is the quality of funding being channelled."

**Unsubsidised RON95 drops 40 sen to RM3.87 a litre this week, its lowest since early September.**

The Ministry of Finance (MOF) cut RON97 and diesel too, until Nov 18. MOF says fuel prices around the region fell in the week it uses to set the price, after cheaper crude oil and more supply. If you pay the full price or use RON97 or diesel, you'll feel it at the petrol pump.

**Malaysia's unemployment rate held at 2.9% in September, the same as August.**

That's the share of the workforce without a job, from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM). Slightly more people were working than in August, and the rate for July to September didn't move either. DOSM says the jobs market is growing steadily and wants more AI and automation training for workers.

**Oil bounced back from a two-month low on Wednesday as ship attacks in the Strait of Hormuz cooled hopes of a truce in the Iran war.**

Reuters says attacks show little sign of slowing. Even with a deal, analysts it quotes say more oil flowing would depend on clearing mines, insurance costs and whether ship owners take the risk. MOF's fuel statement warned world prices will keep swinging while the conflict involving Iran is unresolved.
