*3-minute read*

Malaysia's [exports](/explainers/malaysia-exports/) to Taiwan, the goods we sell there, jumped 88% to RM116.58 billion from January to September, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told Parliament on Thursday, as he confirmed that economic talks with Taiwan planned for October were postponed. Those talks are about Taiwanese investment in Malaysia, which Anwar says is still going into chip and AI factories here, the kind behind our exports and the jobs in them.

![Photo of rows of black computer chips on a green circuit board, seen up close.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-13/photo-taiwan-malaysia-investment-2026-11-13.jpg)

It's Malaysia's answer to Taiwan's foreign ministry, which confirmed on Tuesday that it had paused investment talks after Anwar's August remarks on Taiwan. Asked in Parliament whether ties had been hit, he answered "No", at least "as of now".

Taiwan was Malaysia's fourth-biggest trading partner last year, and we buy even more from Taiwan than we sell to it, RM137.67 billion of goods from January to September. That trade is part of a wider export boom, which in September saw Malaysia sell more than RM200 billion of goods abroad in a single month for the first time.

Anwar says checks by the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) found Taiwanese companies here running smoothly. Malaysia has also asked Taiwan to restart the talks, no date yet. He said the One China policy, Malaysia's recognition of Beijing, will not change, while opposition leader Hamzah Zainudin said his worry is Malaysia's trade and diplomatic interests.

If a Taiwanese company holds back a Malaysian project, Anwar's "no" gets harder to stand on. The first check comes on Friday, Nov 20, when the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) releases October's trade figures, sales to Taiwan included.

## Also worth knowing

**Malaysia's main share index fell for a fourth day on Thursday, to its lowest level this year.**

The [FBM KLCI](/explainers/klci/) ended at 1,585.96, with many more shares falling than rising across Bursa. Mohd Sedek Jantan, an analyst at IPPFA, points to worries that AI shares are overpriced, a tougher-sounding US central bank and stalled US-Iran talks. Thong Pak Leng of Rakuten Trade thinks the fall could soon bring in bargain hunters.

**A senior US Federal Reserve official said more US interest rate rises will likely be needed.**

Mary Daly heads the San Francisco branch of the [Fed](/explainers/federal-reserve/) (the US central bank). On Thursday she said: "My bias is that further tightening will be needed." Bets on a December rise are what analysts pointed to this week for pressure on Malaysian shares and the ringgit. The Fed decides on Thursday, Dec 10, Malaysia time.

**Oil prices rose on Thursday after US President Donald Trump said the US doesn't need a deal with Iran.**

"We don't need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed," he posted, after calling the war "militarily effectively over" on Monday. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) warned on Wednesday that fuel prices will keep swinging while the Iran conflict lasts, and regional prices set the weekly full price at the petrol pump.

**Nvidia's boss Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday after reports about his private life, one tied to a suspected spy.**

US authorities suspect a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore cloud computing company with a subsidiary in Malaysia, of spying for China, and reports link her to Huang. Megaspeed says it cooperated with US checks on chip exports. Nvidia, the biggest maker of AI chips, is run by finance chief Colette Kress for now.
