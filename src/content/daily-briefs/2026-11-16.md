*3-minute read*

The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), the government agency that counts the economy, puts out its official count of July to September growth on Wednesday, replacing an early estimate of 5.3%. The same day, it shows how much foreign companies put into Malaysia over those three months, after that money dropped to RM7.4 billion in April to June from RM22.8 billion in January to March.

![Photo of a metal part spinning on a lathe in a factory workshop, seen up close.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-16/photo-malaysia-gdp-2026-11-16.jpg)

Growth here means [GDP growth](/explainers/gdp/), basically how much more the whole economy produced than in the same months last year, and it's the number behind jobs and pay. The early 5.3% was a bit slower than the 6.0% from April to June. Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah Azizan said on Sunday he expects 2026 to come in above 5%, which would make it a third straight year above that line.

That foreign money is called [foreign direct investment](/explainers/fdi-malaysia/), and it goes into factories and businesses here. So Wednesday answers two things: whether the official count matches the early 5.3%, and whether that investment picked back up after the drop.

In the early count, factories grew fastest, led by electrical and electronic goods, while mining shrank as less crude oil came out of the ground. Wednesday's full figures will show whether that held.

## Also from the weekend

**The Finance Minister II says the OPR was kept at 2.75% so businesses can keep growing.**

Amir Hamzah said on Sunday that with inflation under control and growth strong, a steady [OPR](/explainers/opr/), the main rate Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) sets for loans, keeps borrowing costs stable. So home and car loan rates stay where they are for now. The decision stays with BNM's rate panel, which next meets on Thursday, Jan 21, 2027.

**Malaysia and the rest of ASEAN signed their first binding agreement on the digital economy on Saturday.**

Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani signed for Malaysia, so its online trade rules cover businesses selling across ASEAN, the 11 Southeast Asian countries. It aims to link payment systems and get e-invoices accepted. The deal is due to take effect in 2027, and ASEAN wants its digital economy to double to US$2 trillion by 2030.

Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney announced it in Manila on Saturday, and Malaysia is one of the ASEAN countries in it. The text and tariff lists, the taxes on imported goods, aren't out yet, so nothing changes for now, and each country still has to approve the deal at home.
