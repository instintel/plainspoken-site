*3-minute read*

Big foreign investors sold a net RM270.2 million of Malaysian shares last week, while everyday local investors bought a net RM326.3 million. Net means what a group bought minus what it sold. Money going into or out of Malaysian shares moves their prices, and those prices sit behind the shares and unit trusts many Malaysians own.

![Photo of a tablet screen showing a green line chart and coloured bars, seen up close at an angle.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-17/photo-bursa-foreign-funds-2026-11-17.jpg)

An investment bank's weekly fund flow report, out on Monday, shows the selling ended two weeks of foreign buying. It covers the week to Friday, Nov 13. By then Bursa Malaysia's main share index, [the KLCI](/explainers/klci/) (a scoreboard of the 30 biggest listed companies), had fallen five days in a row to 1,582.59, what The Edge called a 14-month low.

Technology shares took the biggest hit, with RM241.5 million of net foreign selling.

Malaysian institutions, the big local investors, sold a net RM56 million too. So local retail investors were the only group buying, for the 12th week in a row.

Meanwhile, across the eight Asian markets in the report, foreign investors went back to buying overall after six weeks of selling. Malaysia was one of the markets they still sold.

On Monday the KLCI rose 0.34% to 1,588.03, ending the losing run, though more shares fell than rose. Thong Pak Leng, a Rakuten Trade analyst, said more attractive prices could draw more bargain hunters. [The ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) weakened against the US dollar, to 4.0915 from 4.0845, on bets of another US interest rate rise, said Bank Muamalat chief economist Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid.

Thong named results from Nvidia, the US AI chip giant, due early on Wednesday, Nov 18, Malaysia time, and this week's Malaysian economic figures as the next things to watch.

## Other things worth knowing

**The US now wants licences for advanced AI chips shipped to Malaysia.**

The US Commerce Department named Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil for "a higher-than-normal risk of diversion". US-headquartered buyers are exempt if each shipment is reported, and data centres anywhere serving Chinese companies at scale need licences too. The rule isn't in force yet. [Data centres](/explainers/data-centres/) were RM95.8 billion of Malaysia's approved investment from January to June, says the Malaysian Investment Development Authority.

**Chinese shoppers and factories slowed more than expected in October.**

Sales at China's shops rose just 0.4% from a year earlier, down from September's pace, China's statistics office said on Monday. Factory output growth slowed too, and both came in below what economists in a Reuters poll expected. China is one of Malaysia's biggest export markets.

**Most Malaysian businesses expect their costs to keep rising, an HSBC survey finds.**

In the bank's survey, 80% of the Malaysian firms it asked expect costs to rise further over the next two years, a bigger share than businesses globally. Freight, raw materials and energy are the costs they mention most.

**MediAsas, a standard medical insurance plan starting in January, will cover up to RM100,000 of claims a year, with a higher limit for older policyholders.**

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) deputy governor Aznan Abdul Aziz said that limit resets every year. The plan is aimed at Malaysians with no medical insurance and those squeezed by rising premiums. He said premiums can still change later because medical costs keep rising, and none have been published yet.
