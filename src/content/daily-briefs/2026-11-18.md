*3-minute read*

Malaysia gave the go-ahead to RM350.4 billion of investment projects from January to September, 20.7% more than the same months last year, and they're expected to bring 152,090 jobs once the projects are carried out. The count came out on Tuesday from the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA), the government agency that puts out these figures.

![Photo of construction workers in hard hats on a grid of steel bars at a building site, seen from above.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-18/photo-approved-investment-2026-11-18.jpg)

Approved means a company has the go-ahead to build a factory or another project here. The money is spent later, if the projects get built. Manufacturing projects alone carry 92,489 of those expected jobs, 27.3% more than a year ago. And MIDA says 87.4% of the factory projects it approved from 2021 to September are already producing, building or putting in machines.

Foreign companies drove the rise. Their approved projects jumped 40.7% to RM212.3 billion, while Malaysian companies' own approvals fell 1% to RM138.1 billion.

The US topped the foreign list with RM60.0 billion, more than five times the RM11.3 billion of a year earlier and more than a quarter of all approved foreign investment. China (RM38.6 billion) and Singapore (RM38.5 billion) came next.

![The US leads Malaysia's approved foreign investment so far in 2026 (Chart). Approved foreign investment by source country, January to September 2026, in RM billion. The five biggest sources.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-18/chart-approved-foreign-investment-2026-11-18.png)

Nine months of 2026 already come to 81.3% of the RM431.1 billion approved in the whole of 2025. Malaysia's economy grew 5.3% from July to September, on the early estimate from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM).

Later today DOSM gives its official growth figure, and its count of how much [foreign direct investment](/explainers/fdi-malaysia/) actually arrived in those three months, the money that came in rather than the plans.

## Also on the radar

**Malaysia's main share index fell to 1,575.15 as tech and data centre shares dropped.**

A Rakuten Trade analyst pointed to Monday night's US share fall over Nvidia, the AI chip company, and tighter US rules on advanced chip exports. Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani said Malaysia has no plans for US talks, and [data centre](/explainers/data-centres/) firms must comply: "If you want to be in this game, you have to follow the regulation."

**Malaysia and France are now strategic partners, with a rare earths project due to start in early 2027.**

The plan has a French company and a Malaysian company mining and processing the minerals in Malaysia, with more refining in France. The seven documents exchanged during French President Emmanuel Macron's visit on Tuesday also cover energy and a first step toward buying Airbus rescue helicopters. France already has more than 300 companies here.

**The ringgit held still against the US dollar but weakened against its neighbours' currencies.**

It stayed at 4.0915 per US dollar on Tuesday but was weaker against the Singapore dollar and the Thai baht, so a trip next door costs a little more. Bank Muamalat chief economist Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid said expectations of a half-point rate rise from [the Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/), the US central bank, next month would give the US dollar strong support.

**Nvidia's sales more than doubled to US$115.8 billion in its first results since its boss quit.**

That beat what analysts expected, and its shares rose in trading after the US market closed. It expects more next quarter, and that outlook assumes no data centre chip sales to China. The results came out after Malaysia's share market closed on Tuesday.
