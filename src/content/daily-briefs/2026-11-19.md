*3-minute read*

Malaysia's economy, the one your job and pay depend on, grew 5.5% from July to September, more than the 5.3% first estimated in October, and spending on new buildings and machines grew even faster.

![Photo of tower cranes over a building site, in silhouette against an evening sky.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-19/photo-malaysia-gdp-2026-11-19.jpg)

That's the official count of [GDP](/explainers/gdp/), the total value of everything Malaysia's economy produces, from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), measured against the same months last year. It's slower than the 6.0% of April to June, but the first nine months of 2026 come to 5.6%, up from 4.8% a year earlier.

![Malaysia's economy grew 5.5% from July to September (Chart). Real GDP growth each quarter since January to March 2024, against the same quarter a year earlier, in percent. The latest bar is DOSM's official figure.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-19/chart-malaysia-gdp-growth-2026-11-19.png)

On Tuesday, Malaysia's count of [approved investment](/explainers/approved-investment/) reached RM350.4 billion for the year so far, and those are plans. Wednesday's figures show money actually being spent, and investment spending grew 8.1%, faster than the 4.6% of April to June. Foreign companies also put a net RM14.3 billion into Malaysia, nearly double the RM7.4 billion of the quarter before.

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) expects investment to be supported by projects that run over several years and by approved investments being carried out.

Household spending, though, grew more slowly, at 4.3% from 4.8%. BNM says the economy grew more slowly than in April to June because cash handouts were bigger in the same months of 2025, which makes this year's growth look slower by comparison.

BNM says the economy is on pace to grow more than 5% this year. DOSM's next check, its early estimate for October to December, is expected in January.

## Other things that moved

**Malaysia's main share index fell to 1,566.73 as investors sold shares in a few big companies.**

More shares rose than fell on Bursa Malaysia, the stock market, but selling in big names like Maybank and Public Bank pulled [the KLCI](/explainers/klci/) down. Those prices feed many Malaysians' unit trusts and Employees Provident Fund savings. Rakuten Trade's Thong Pak Leng said the GDP figures gave some comfort, yet the selling suggests "investor confidence has yet to recover".

**BNM says the new US chip rules shouldn't hit data centre investment here in a big way.**

The US has issued a rule requiring a licence for advanced AI chips sent to Malaysia. Governor Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour said it's too early to judge the full impact, but only 17 of the 120 data centre companies here are Chinese-owned. He sees that investment, which brings building and electronics work, staying strong until at least 2028.

**Full-price RON95 drops to RM3.72 a litre from today, down from RM3.87, with diesel and RON97 cheaper too.**

That's the price for drivers not on BUDI95. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) said refined fuel prices in the region fell, in line with lower world crude oil prices, but warned that disruptions to fuel shipments could still hit supplies. BUDI95 and BUDI Diesel prices stay the same.

**The ringgit weakened to 4.1130 against the US dollar as the outlook for higher US interest rates weighed.**

It was 4.0915 on Tuesday, and a higher number means a weaker ringgit, so anything priced in US dollars, from imports to overseas fees, now costs a little more in ringgit. Bank Muamalat Malaysia chief economist Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid said attention is now on the US central bank's December meeting.
