*3-minute read*

What it costs you to get around pushed Malaysia's [inflation](/explainers/inflation/) up to 1.9% in October, from 1.8% the month before. Transport prices rose 4.2% from a year earlier, up from 2.5% in September, and the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) says transport drove the rise.

![Photo of evening traffic on a winding road, shown as streaks of car lights.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-20/photo-malaysia-inflation-2026-11-20.jpg)

Inflation is how much more the same everyday things cost than a year ago. DOSM tracks it every month with the consumer price index, which follows the prices of a basket of things households spend on. At 1.9%, a mix of everyday spending that cost RM100 last October cost about RM101.90 this October, so your ringgit buys a little less than it did a year ago.

Transport prices rose more than twice as fast as prices overall. Home bills, the group that covers housing, electricity, water and gas, also sped up, to 1.9% from 1.4%. Food went the other way, rising 1.7%, a bit slower than September's 1.8%.

Still, the overall rise came in just under the 2.0% that economists polled by Bloomberg expected. And the rate has stayed between 1.3% and 2.0% every month since October 2025.

![Malaysia's inflation rose to 1.9% in October (Chart). How much prices in Malaysia rose each month against the same month a year earlier, in percent, October 2025 to October 2026. The latest bar is DOSM's October figure.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-20/chart-malaysia-inflation-2026-11-20.png)

Inflation also comes into economists' views on your loan rates. The [Overnight Policy Rate (OPR)](/explainers/opr/) is the main interest rate Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) sets, and banks build loan rates on it. BNM kept it at 2.75% on Nov 5. On Wednesday, before these figures, HSBC and ANZ economists said they expect a rise early next year, with ANZ saying strong growth with low inflation "is unlikely to last". Kenanga said a rise may not be needed if inflation stays low and growth slows.

DOSM's November figures will show whether October's transport jump was a one-off.

## Also worth knowing

**Malaysia's main share index ended almost flat as bank shares bounced back late.**

[The KLCI](/explainers/klci/), Bursa Malaysia's scoreboard of its biggest companies, closed 0.57 points lower at 1,566.16 after late buying in Maybank, Public Bank and RHB. Those prices feed many Malaysians' unit trusts and Employees Provident Fund (EPF) savings. Rakuten Trade's Thong Pak Leng tied a recent fall in data centre shares to doubts about their future earnings after tighter US restrictions.

**Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim says China-based companies have proposed RM9.2 billion of investment in Malaysia.**

The proposals came out of meetings during his trip to a summit of Asia-Pacific leaders in Shenzhen, China, and cover projects in Kedah, Penang, Selangor and Pahang. Anwar said the figure is still indicative, and the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) will share the details later.

**The ringgit firmed a little against the US dollar after Malaysia's stronger growth figures.**

The ringgit ended Thursday at 4.1100, from 4.1130 the day before, and a lower number means a stronger ringgit. Bernama put it down to positive market sentiment after Wednesday's figures showed stronger growth in Malaysia's economy. That makes anything priced in US dollars, from imports to overseas fees, a little cheaper in ringgit.

**US share prices fell for a sixth day in a row on worries about the AI boom.**

The S&P 500, a measure of 500 big US companies, dropped 2% on Thursday, its longest losing run since 2024. The AP news agency says investors doubt AI can earn enough, and the sudden resignation of Nvidia's boss, Jensen Huang, added to the worry. Even AI company Anthropic's strong first day of trading didn't lift the market.
