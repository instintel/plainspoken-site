*3-minute read*

Japan's and South Korea's main share indexes each fell more than 5% on Friday as AI shares sold off, and Malaysia's main share index, the [KLCI](/explainers/klci/), got off lighter, down 0.61%. But for anyone holding Malaysian shares or a unit trust, that was the KLCI's fourth fall in a row. It left the index at 1,556.51, below last Friday's 1,582.59, which local business paper The Edge called a 14-month low.

![Photo of the Kuala Lumpur skyline, rows of office towers under a cloudy sky.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-21/photo-klci-2026-11-21.jpg)

The KLCI is the scoreboard of the 30 biggest companies on Bursa Malaysia, the country's stock exchange, so it's a quick read on how Malaysia's biggest listed companies are doing. This week it lost 1.65%.

![The KLCI fell four days in a row to 1,556.51 (Chart). Malaysia's main share index, the FBM KLCI, at each day's close since Oct 27, in index points. The shading marks this week, Nov 16 to 20.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-21/chart-klci-daily-close-2026-11-21.png)

On Friday 837 shares fell and only 325 rose. Bernama put it down to persistent selling amid the global share sell-off, particularly in big data centre-related shares, and technology shares were among the biggest losers. Thong Pak Leng, an analyst at Rakuten Trade, said the continued selling suggests investor confidence "has yet to recover".

Then, hours after Bursa closed, US shares had a much worse day. The S&P 500, a share index of 500 big US companies, fell 3.7% and ended more than 10% below its Oct 6 record, a drop traders call a correction, the first since March 2025. The AP news agency said investors worry that AI companies' values and heavy spending can't be justified.

For context, Malaysia's economy grew 5.5% from July to September, the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) said on Nov 18.

Bursa's next session is on Monday, its first since the US correction. A close back above last Friday's 1,582.59 would undo this week's slide.

## Elsewhere on Friday

**Malaysia's exports jumped 61.1% in October, and total trade passed RM400 billion in a month for the first time.**

That's against October last year, DOSM said, and Bank Muamalat chief economist Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid said it beat the consensus forecast. [Electrical and electronic products](/explainers/electronics-exports/), the chips and parts Malaysian factories make, gave the biggest lift. Malaysia's trade ministry put the electronics rise down to steady global demand for semiconductors and growing use of AI.

**The ringgit barely moved against the US dollar as traders waited for the US central bank's December meeting.**

It ended Friday at 4.1105, from 4.1100 (a lower number means a stronger ringgit). Afzanizam expects the mood to stay cautious until the [Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/) meets on Dec 8 and 9. The ringgit firmed against the euro and the pound, but the US dollar rate sets what anything priced in US dollars costs you.

**Palm oil prices fell to their lowest close since March on reports that Chinese buyers cancelled shipments.**

Bursa Malaysia's benchmark palm oil price ended Friday at RM4,217 a tonne, the lowest close since March 5, Reuters reported. Bernama cited those cancellations, high stocks at Chinese ports and Malaysia's record stockpiles. Palm oil is one of Malaysia's main farm exports, and those earnings were lower in October than a year earlier.

**Brent crude fell for a third week as traders weighed plentiful oil supply against the risk of new US strikes on Iran.**

Brent, the world oil price everyone quotes, ended Friday at US$92.57 a barrel, Bloomberg reported. The International Energy Agency expects the world to have more oil than it uses this quarter. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets the weekly full price of petrol and diesel from regional fuel prices that follow world crude.

## What to watch

- **Wednesday November 25:** MOF's weekly fuel prices for Nov 26 to Dec 2, usually out on Wednesday, showing whether lower oil prices reach the full price at the petrol pump.
- **Wednesday November 25:** US inflation for October on the PCE measure, a check on how fast US prices are rising before the Fed meets.
- **Friday November 27:** DOSM's October trade price and volume figures, showing how much of the export jump came from shipping more goods.
- **Thursday December 3:** The KLCI's December review results, naming the 20 companies joining as it grows from 30 to 50.
- **Thursday December 10:** The Fed's rate decision from its Dec 8 to 9 meeting, early morning Malaysia time, showing whether US interest rates go up again.
