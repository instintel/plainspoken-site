*3-minute read*

The US puts out its [October inflation figure](/explainers/us-inflation/) at 9:30pm Malaysia time on Wednesday, and forecasters' estimates put it at 3.8%, up from 3.6% in September. Bank Muamalat chief economist Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid, who cited those estimates on Saturday, says [ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) traders will be watching it closely. US figures like this can give clues on the next move in US interest rates, what it costs to borrow there, the national news agency Bernama reported. Your ringgit is what sets the cost of anything you pay for in US dollars, and it ended last week weaker, at 4.1105 to the US dollar from 4.0845 a week earlier.

![Photo of a shopping trolley in a supermarket.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-23/photo-us-inflation-2026-11-23.jpg)

Wednesday's number is PCE inflation, basically how fast prices for the things US households buy are rising compared with a year earlier. The US central bank aims to keep it at 2%, so September's 3.6% is still above that goal.

That central bank, [the Federal Reserve (the Fed)](/explainers/federal-reserve/), held the band its main interest rate sits in at 3.75% to 4.00% in October, though two of its members wanted a rise. This week's figure is the last big US inflation reading before it decides again.

A reading of 3.8% would be in line with those estimates. One at or below September's 3.6% would mean US prices didn't speed up after all. The same release also carries a second look at how fast the US economy grew from July to September.

Fed officials announce that decision at 3am on Thursday, Dec 10, Malaysia time.

## Over the weekend

**Apex Securities expects Malaysia's inflation to pick up to 2.0% in 2027.**

The broker has raised its oil price assumption and expects businesses to pass higher costs on to shoppers bit by bit, so prices rise modestly from this year's pace. Apex says that backs its call for Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) to raise the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR), the rate banks build your loan rates on, next year.

**Barisan Nasional's chairman says BN would bring back GST immediately if it wins back federal power.**

Ahmad Zahid Hamidi, who chairs the BN coalition in the federal government, said on Sunday GST, the goods and services tax charged at 6% until 2018, is fairer than today's sales and service tax (SST). Both tax what you buy; he gave no rate or date. A Kedah consumer group asked for half the old rate, with necessities exempt.

**Bahrain says an Iranian attack hit an industrial site there on Saturday, wounding two people.**

Residents saw smoke over Sitra Island, home to a refinery run by Bahrain's state oil company Bapco, the AFP news agency reported. Oil didn't trade over the weekend. The Ministry of Finance (MOF), which sets the full petrol price each week from regional fuel prices, said on Nov 18 that trouble abroad and delivery risks could still affect supply.

**The cost of living ministry is lining up shops in flood-prone areas to hold more price-controlled goods.**

Minister Armizan Mohd Ali said on Saturday that his Ministry of Domestic Trade and Cost of Living (KPDN) is watching essential goods' supplies and prices more closely through the monsoon, to keep basics on shelves where floods hit. Festive-style price controls there, he said, depend on what's needed.
