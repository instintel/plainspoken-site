*3-minute read*

Malaysia's main share index, the [KLCI](/explainers/klci/), closed lower for a fifth day in a row on Monday, and a weekly count showed [foreign funds](/explainers/foreign-fund-flows/) sold RM279.2 million more Malaysian shares than they bought last week, their second week of selling. The KLCI tracks the 30 biggest companies on Bursa Malaysia, the country's stock exchange, so a slide like this hits Malaysian shares and the funds that hold them.

![Photo of a glass office tower seen from below against a cloudy sky.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-24/photo-klci-2026-11-24.jpg)

It has closed lower every day since last Monday's 1,588.03:

- Nov 17: 1,575.15
- Nov 18: 1,566.73
- Nov 19: 1,566.16
- Nov 20: 1,556.51
- Nov 23: 1,549.05

That's 38.98 points, about 2.45%, in five trading days.

![The KLCI closed lower five days in a row, to 1,549.05 (Chart). Malaysia's main share index, the FBM KLCI, at each day's close since Oct 27, in index points. The shading marks five lower closes in a row, Nov 17 to 23.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-24/chart-klci-five-day-slide-2026-11-24.png)

On Monday 849 shares fell and 293 rose. Bernama said selling in some of the biggest shares, particularly utilities, weighed on the index. Technology shares bounced a little, but Thong Pak Leng, an analyst at Rakuten Trade, an online share broker, said the bounce wasn't a sign investors feel better.

That count, from MBSB Investment Bank, which tracks who buys and sells Bursa shares, follows RM270.2 million the week before, more than half a billion ringgit in two weeks. Local institutions bought a net RM232.3 million last week, and ordinary local investors bought RM46.9 million, their 13th week of buying in a row.

Across eight Asian markets, MBSB counted about US$6.3 billion of foreign money leaving shares that week, and only India drew more foreign buying than selling.

Malaysia's economy itself grew 5.5% from July to September, the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) said on Nov 18.

Thong expects prices to keep swinging as investors rethink global technology prospects, and said a lasting recovery needs stronger buying. A close back above last Monday's 1,588.03 would undo the slide.

## Also moving on Monday

**Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said the government will improve incentives for aircraft repair firms, which earned RM13 billion last year.**

That's the part of Malaysia's aerospace industry that services and repairs aircraft, and it employs thousands of skilled workers here. Anwar said it after a government investment council met on Monday, adding that the government machinery must act swiftly to help investors. The meeting also reviewed progress on an industrial park in Kerian, Perak.

**The ringgit weakened against the US dollar as investors braced for the US central bank to stay tough on interest rates.**

It ended Monday at 4.1205, from 4.1105 on Friday (a higher number means a weaker ringgit), so US-dollar prices cost a bit more in ringgit. Bernama said investors expect the [Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/) to keep that stance next month. Bank Muamalat economist Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid said investors will watch Wednesday's US inflation report.

**Oil prices fell after Trump signalled more time for talks with Iran.**

Bloomberg said investors undid trades made in case US forces stepped up fighting in Iran, though Trump told Fox News he may resume the war next year if talks fail. Reuters pointed to more Middle East oil and a Libyan pipeline restarting. The Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets the weekly full petrol and diesel price from regional fuel prices that follow world crude.

**US shares had their best day since April 2025 after Nvidia brought back Jensen Huang as its boss.**

The S&P 500, an index of 500 big US companies, rose 4.7%, winning back just over half of last week's loss, the AP news agency said. Nvidia, the US AI chip company, said its board had input from senior US government officials and is confident Huang's return poses no national security risk. Its own investigation continues.
