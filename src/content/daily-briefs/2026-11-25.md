*3-minute read*

Malaysia's government says it will tell every [data centre](/explainers/data-centres/) in the country not to host, rent space to or serve seven firms the US has just put on a trade blacklist, and two of them are Malaysian companies. Data centres are buildings packed with computers that companies rent to store data and run AI, and Malaysia has approved a lot of them: 27 projects worth RM225.97 billion between February 2025 and July 2026, the Ministry of Digital said in October.

![Photo of server racks with green and blue lights in a dark server room.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-25/photo-data-centre-2026-11-25.jpg)

On Monday the US Commerce Department added the seven, based in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, to its Entity List, citing a "significant risk" of them passing advanced computing goods on to others. Anyone sending them US-regulated items will need a US licence, which the rule says will usually be refused. The two Malaysian names are Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd, owned by Singapore-registered Megaspeed International, and MSFusion Sdn Bhd.

On Tuesday the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) said the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA), the government's investment agency, will send the notice to all data centre operators. Malaysia "will not allow itself to be used as a channel to circumvent export controls", MITI said, and anyone found breaking the law will face action. MIDA's records show Megaspeed isn't a company in Malaysia and doesn't run or rent space in any data centre here, it added.

That sits on top of a rule Malaysia already has. Since July 2025, sending high-end US-made AI chips out of Malaysia or through it needs a permit, though bringing them in doesn't.

Wednesday is when the US rule is due in the Federal Register, the US government's official journal.

## Elsewhere on Tuesday

**The [KLCI](/explainers/klci/), Malaysia's main share index, ended five days of falls in a row.**

It rose to 1,561.68 from 1,549.05, winning back about a third of the five-day drop, which lifts shares you own directly or through a unit trust. Bernama tied it to upbeat regional markets after Nvidia, the US AI chip company, brought back boss Jensen Huang. A Rakuten Trade analyst said it was too early to say the mood had fundamentally improved.

**The [ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) gained on the US dollar, and an economist called a US rate rise in December "almost a certainty".**

It ended Tuesday at 4.1135, from 4.1205 (a lower number means a stronger ringgit), so US-dollar prices cost a little less. Bank Muamalat economist Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid also said the ringgit looks oversold, so further falls seem limited. The [Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/), the US central bank, decides early on Thursday, Dec 10, Malaysia time.

**Sabah expects an extra RM900 million from the federal government by the end of November.**

State Finance Minister Datuk Seri Masidi Manjun said it would strengthen Sabah's finances if it comes on time, and it's promised before the state assembly sitting from Dec 4 ends. Oil and gas bring in about half of Sabah's income, and since the state sells oil in US dollars, he said a strong ringgit doesn't always mean more money.

**US shares fell as AI stocks gave back part of Monday's surge.**

The S&P 500, an index of 500 big US companies, fell 0.5% on Tuesday, its eighth loss in nine days, the AP news agency said. Monday's AI rally "quickly fizzled", AP said, with Nvidia falling back, though most other shares rose and the Dow, another US share index, ended higher. US markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving, a US public holiday.
