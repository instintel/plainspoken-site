*3-minute read*

The US has proposed a tariff of up to 18% on all goods Malaysia sells it, the same rate it named for Indonesia, Thailand and Cambodia, and well under Vietnam's 35%. A tariff is a tax the US charges on goods as they arrive, so this is a possible extra charge on Malaysian-made goods landing there.

![Photo of a ship loaded with stacked shipping containers, seen from above at sea.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-26/photo-us-tariff-2026-11-26.jpg)

And the US is no small customer. It took RM282.07 billion of Malaysian goods from January to September, 69.1% more than a year earlier, mostly electronics, and computer chips alone are 44% of those. Everything Malaysia sold abroad in October was up 61.1% on a year before.

![Malaysia's proposed US tariff matches three neighbours (Chart). The highest US tariff rate proposed for each South-East Asian economy on Nov 25, 2026, in per cent. These are proposals, not yet in force.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-26/chart-us-tariff-asean-2026-11-26.png)

The number came out of a check-up. USTR, the US government office that runs US trade policy, used a US trade law, Section 301, to look at 14 economies for what it calls excess factory capacity. For Malaysia, it pointed to plans to grow factories and exports, special financing from national funding programmes and weak labour protections, among other things. In plain words, the US has found fault with some of the ways Malaysia helps its factories grow.

Still, what landed on Wednesday is basically a quotation. The invoice only comes after hearings, if it comes at all. And some line items are already crossed out. Trade lawyers reading the notices say goods under separate US tariffs, including computer chips, are left out. Remember those chips from earlier.

There's a stricter version too. Bernama reports USTR could go up to 25% if it judges Malaysia's talks and trade commitments to the US aren't good enough.

If you or your employer sells to the US, check whether your product sits in that left-out group or in the two areas USTR flagged for Malaysia, information and communication technology, and cars. Written comments close on Tuesday, Jan 5, 2027, and hearings start in Washington on Monday, Jan 25. That's where the quotation gets argued over, before anyone sends an invoice.

## Four more things from Wednesday

**Bursa Malaysia ended slightly higher while share markets in Japan and South Korea fell hard.**

[The KLCI](/explainers/klci/), Bursa's main index, ended at 1,562.29, up 0.61 of a point. Small, but up on a day Japan and South Korea were down, with buyers picking big consumer, plantation and transport names. More shares still fell than rose. Thong Pak Leng of Rakuten Trade sees a possible hit to local chip and data-centre shares' earnings outlook and prices.

**US inflation on the Federal Reserve's favourite measure rose to 3.8% in October.**

That's [the PCE index](/explainers/us-inflation/), the price gauge the Fed (the US central bank) watches most, and it moved up from 3.6% in September. The core reading, without food and energy, also came in a little higher than economists expected. Yahoo Finance says that firmed up bets on a US rate rise, and the Fed gets to decide on Thursday, Dec 10.

**Full-price RON95, RON97 and diesel are 25 sen a litre cheaper this week.**

The Ministry of Finance (MOF) cut [full-price RON95](/explainers/ron95-price/) to RM3.47, from RM3.72. Its reasons came from far beyond the petrol station, with regional fuel prices going down and more crude oil expected worldwide. If you fill up at full price or run a diesel van, that's 25 sen less on every litre, though MOF says prices may keep moving.

**US shares won back most of a steep early fall, and the S&P 500 ended just 0.3% lower.**

The S&P 500, an index of 500 big US companies, had been down as much as 3.2%, so the close only looked calm if you missed the middle. AP linked the fall to a second day of losses in AI shares, with an inflation report and the trade-war plan adding to the worries.

- **Thursday December 3:** The December review of the KLCI names the companies joining it. Which firms get into Malaysia's main share index as it grows from 30 to 50 companies.
- **Thursday December 10, 3:00am MYT:** The US Federal Reserve's rate decision. Whether the US central bank raises its interest rate.
- **Tuesday January 5, 2027:** Deadline for written comments on USTR's tariff plan. The last day to put a case to the US before the hearings.
- **Monday January 25, 2027:** USTR's public hearings begin in Washington. Where the proposal gets argued out before any decision.
