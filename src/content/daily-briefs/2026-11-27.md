*3-minute read*

"The ayes have it," the Deputy Dewan Rakyat Speaker said late on Thursday afternoon, and with that the Dewan Rakyat passed Budget 2027, a RM510 billion plan, on a voice vote, the kind where nobody counts heads.

![Photo of Malaysian sen coins stacked and scattered on a dark wooden table.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-27/photo-budget-2027-2026-11-27.jpg)

The Budget is the government's plan for what it spends and borrows in 2027, built around [a deficit](/explainers/fiscal-deficit/), the shortfall it borrows to cover, of 3.3% of the size of the economy, down from an estimated 3.6% this year. And it carries the stuff that touches your own money. As presented on Oct 9:

- Individual tax relief goes up from RM9,000 to RM12,000, the first change since 2010, and the government says about 5 million taxpayers could keep up to RM1,600 more.
- STR and SARA cash aid rises to RM16 billion, from RM15 billion.
- [The minimum wage](/explainers/minimum-wage/) goes from RM1,700 to RM2,000 a month from June 2027, with smaller firms selling under RM50 million a year exempt.

Still, think of it like a housing loan. The branch has said yes, but nothing is final until head office signs off and the papers are stamped. Here, the Dewan Negara debates and approves the Budget next, then it needs royal assent, with no dates given yet for either.

Meanwhile, the RM510 billion, up from the RM470 billion announced for Budget 2026, isn't all the government spending its own money:

- RM376.8 billion is operating spending, the cost of running the government day to day. That's about four in every five ringgit of its own RM459.8 billion.
- RM83 billion is development spending, the money for new projects.
- RM50.2 billion is investment by GLICs, public-private partnerships and other government-linked bodies, outside the government's own allocation.

![Most of Budget 2027's RM510 billion goes to running costs (Chart). Budget 2027 as passed by the Dewan Rakyat on Nov 26, 2026, in RM billion. The third bar is investment by GLICs and other government-linked bodies, outside the federal allocation.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-27/chart-budget-2027-split-2026-11-27.png)

If the Dewan Negara sends it back, or the government changes its 3.3% target, the timing and the sums change. Until then, the vote that counted no heads still needs two more signatures before Budget 2027 is done.

## Also moving on Thursday

**Bursa's main index, the KLCI, rose as buyers went for big bank and consumer shares.**

It ended at 1,570.98, up 8.69 points. Bernama put it down to buying in banks and consumer names, and Thong Pak Leng of Rakuten Trade said easing US government bond rates lifted Asian markets. More shares still fell than rose, so the big names did the lifting. They're the shares many unit trusts and Employees Provident Fund (EPF) savings hold.

**Carmaker BYD will start putting together its first car in Malaysia, the Shark 6 pick-up, in Kulim.**

The pick-ups will be assembled at Sime Motors' Inokom plant in Kedah. BYD Malaysia's Jacob Ma expects the first units early next year, by the first quarter at the latest. Nobody gave an investment amount, a job count or how many trucks it will make.

**Malaysia will keep negotiating with the US until early 2027 to cap its total tariff at 19%.**

That's the rate in Malaysia's trade deal with the US, and Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani wants every extra US charge on Malaysian goods kept inside it. "You promised us a 19 per cent tariff rate," he said. Computer chips stay out of the US check "at this stage".

**Oil climbed above US$95 a barrel after attack claims around Saudi Arabia and the Strait of Hormuz.**

Yemen's Houthis said they hit Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's biggest refinery, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two tankers struck sea mines off Oman. Saudi Arabia's military said its air defences destroyed four drones aimed at the refinery. World oil prices feed the weekly full fuel price the Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets, next due on Wednesday.
