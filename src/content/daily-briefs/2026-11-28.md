*3-minute read*

Bursa Malaysia's main share index, [the KLCI](/explainers/klci/), rose 1.16% on Friday to 1,589.24, which wins back its whole five-day slide. It now sits above 1,588.03, where it ended on Monday Nov 16, before five straight falls took it down to 1,549.05.

![Photo of office and apartment towers rising behind a city park with palm trees and a pond.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-28/photo-klci-2026-11-28.jpg)

The KLCI is a scoreboard of Malaysia's biggest listed companies, and many unit trusts and Employees Provident Fund (EPF) savings hold those same shares. So the index your savings may lean on has just finished a round trip: five days down, four days back up, and home again with a little change to spare.

In numbers, the trip went like this:

- Down: from 1,588.03 on Nov 16 to 1,549.05 on Nov 23, a loss of 38.98 points.
- Back up: four higher closes in a row, adding 40.19 points.
- Friday to Friday: up 32.73 points, about 2.1%.

![The KLCI fell five days in a row, then won it all back (Chart). Malaysia's main share index, the FBM KLCI, at each day's close since Oct 27, in index points. The shading marks five lower closes in a row, then four higher closes.](/images/daily-briefs/2026-11-28/chart-klci-round-trip-2026-11-28.png)

Meanwhile, the backdrop sat still. Bank Negara Malaysia's (BNM) main interest rate, [the OPR](/explainers/opr/), has stayed at 2.75%, and the economy grew 5.5% in the third quarter, the latest reading.

As for Friday itself, Bernama said Bursa rose with upbeat markets around the region, as buyers went for big industrial and utility names. Three of them, Press Metal Aluminium, YTL Power and YTL Corp, added 12.66 of the 18.26 points, about two-thirds of the day. Three companies doing most of the work is the office group project all over again.

Still, the rest of the market wasn't just watching. Across Bursa, 727 shares rose and 363 fell, the reverse of Thursday, when more fell than rose. Thong Pak Leng of Rakuten Trade said buying spread to mid-sized companies, and pointed to company earnings at home holding up.

That round trip doesn't cover the whole month, though. On Nov 2 the KLCI ended at 1,619.51, and a close under 1,549.05 would put it below the slide's low. For now, the trip that began on Nov 16 ends one small step past the front door.

## Elsewhere on Friday

**Prices Malaysian producers charge rose faster in October, led by crude oil and gas.**

The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) said [producer prices](/explainers/producer-price-index/), what mines, factories and farms charge for their goods, rose 14% in October, up from 12% in September. Mining did most of the climbing, mainly crude petroleum. Producer prices measure what producers get paid, which isn't the same as what you pay at the shops.

**Budget 2027 work can start as early as January 1 or 2, the Prime Minister has instructed.**

Government spokesman Fahmi Fadzil said Anwar Ibrahim has instructed that planned work can start as early as Jan 1 or 2, so the government's 2027 money is spent well and on target. That's an early start for a Budget still waiting on the Dewan Negara, the next house of Parliament, and royal assent, with no dates for either.

**Oil had its first weekly rise of November after attacks on tankers and Saudi oil sites.**

Brent, the global oil price everyone quotes, rose about 3% over the week after three weekly falls. Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said its Jazan refinery halted some operations after a projectile hit it on Thursday, killing a worker. World oil prices feed the weekly full fuel price the Ministry of Finance (MOF) sets, next due on Wednesday.

**US shares had their best week in more than a year.**

The S&P 500, the main US share index, rose 5.6% over the week, its best since April 2025, with technology and AI companies leading. On Friday, about four in five of its companies went up. Quite the change of mood for an index that had ended the previous week more than 10% below its record.
