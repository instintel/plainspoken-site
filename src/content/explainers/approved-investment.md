*Updated Nov 18, 2026*

Approved investment is the total value of business projects, by Malaysian and foreign companies, that government ministries and agencies have signed off for Malaysia, counted at approval, before anything is built. From January to September 2026 it came to RM350.4 billion, up 20.7% on a year earlier, the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) said on Nov 17, 2026.

If the projects approved in those nine months go ahead, MIDA expects them to create 152,090 jobs, 92,489 of them in manufacturing.

## Ministries And Agencies Approve The Projects, And MIDA Adds Them Up.

A project gets counted once a ministry or agency gives it a licence, an incentive, a permit, a grant or a soft loan, MIDA says. The count covers factories, services (businesses that sell a service rather than make goods) and the primary sector, such as mining and plantations.

The figure comes out four times a year: for the first three months, the first half, the first nine months and the full year.

## Approval Comes First, And The Money Follows Later.

After approval, a company still has regulatory steps to finish before it starts building, and MIDA says that typically takes 18 to 24 months. Then comes building, and finally production.

Most approved factory projects do go ahead. Of the 5,822 factory projects approved from 2021 to June 2026, more than 86% had reached construction or production, MIDA said on Aug 28, 2026. Another 9.8% were still planning and 3.2% were not carried out.

Money that foreign companies actually bring in is counted separately, by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), as [foreign direct investment](/explainers/fdi-malaysia/), once it has arrived.

## Approvals Reached A Record RM431.1 Billion In 2025, On MIDA's Latest Count.

MIDA revises past years as it goes. It first put 2025 at RM426.7 billion in March 2026 and now puts it at RM431.1 billion. In the same way, 2024 went from RM378.5 billion to RM384.4 billion.

So far in 2026, the RM350.4 billion approved from January to September is already about 81% of the whole of 2025. Foreign investors' share came to RM212.3 billion of it and Malaysian investors' share to RM138.1 billion.

The US was the biggest foreign source, at RM60.0 billion, up from RM11.3 billion in the same months of 2025. MIDA counts this by ultimate source, the home country of the foreign investor that controls the investment, even if the money passes through other countries on the way.

## Tax Break Rules Changed On Mar 1, 2026, And The 2026 Total Has No Date Yet.

Since Mar 1, 2026, factory projects asking for tax breaks have been judged under the New Incentive Framework of the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI). The framework ties each tax break to measurable results, such as developing local workers' skills and using Malaysian suppliers. Services were due to follow, and MIDA says their start date will be announced.

Each of MIDA's last five full-year totals came out in late February or early March, most recently on Mar 6, 2026.
