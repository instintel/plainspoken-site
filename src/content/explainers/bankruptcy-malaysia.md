*Updated Oct 27, 2026*

Bankruptcy in Malaysia is when the High Court declares you unable to pay your debts. A creditor can only ask for it if you owe them at least RM100,000. From then on, the Malaysian Insolvency Department (MdI) handles your case until you're discharged, which means officially cleared.

## The High Court Makes You Bankrupt, And MdI Runs Your Case.

There are two ways in. A creditor, such as a bank you owe, can file a petition at the High Court if the debt is at least RM100,000. You can also file one yourself, and then there's no minimum debt. If the court agrees, it makes an order declaring you bankrupt, and MdI's Director General of Insolvency handles your bankruptcy from there.

## Being Bankrupt Limits Where You Go And How You Work.

MdI lists what changes. You face limits on going abroad. You can't act as a company director, and your freedom to run a business is restricted. You pay regular contributions towards what you owe, and you hand over assets, including your home and car.

## There Are Three Ways Out, And A Faster Lane For Some Groups.

- **Court discharge:** the High Court can discharge you.
- **MdI certificate:** MdI's Director General can discharge you, subject to any creditor's objection.
- **Section 33C discharge:** Section 33C of the Insolvency Act 1967 allows discharge in as little as three years, for those who meet its conditions.

The Fast Track Discharge Programme puts single parents, small-business borrowers, scam victims and buyers of homes in abandoned housing projects first. Eligible applications finish within 36 days if the conditions are met and no creditor objects, and you apply online through MdI's e-Insolvensi portal.

## New Cases Rose For A Second Year In 2025.

MdI recorded 6,776 new bankruptcy cases in 2025, up from 5,977 in 2024 and the highest of the five years from 2021. Personal loans were the biggest cause from January to July 2026, behind 38.4% of new cases. On the way out, the government says its Second Chance Policy recorded 253,457 bankruptcy discharges from 2023 to September 2026.

## Two Changes Are Coming, One With No Start Date Yet.

- **Oct 9, 2026:** Budget 2027 proposed raising the bankruptcy threshold to RM150,000, from RM100,000. No start date has been announced.
- **2027:** Law Minister Azalina Othman Said told the Dewan Rakyat on Oct 26, 2026 that the government aims to cut fast-track discharge to under 36 days next year.
