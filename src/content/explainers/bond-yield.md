*Updated Nov 7, 2026*

A bond yield is the yearly return you earn for lending money by buying a bond. On Nov 6, 2026, Malaysia's 10-year government bond yield was 3.82%, about one percentage point above the main interest rate set by Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM). A buyer that day would earn about 3.82% a year by holding the bond until the government pays it back. The 10-year yield is also a guide to what it costs the government to borrow for ten years.

The bond itself is an IOU you can buy and sell: you lend money, the borrower pays you interest along the way, and you get your money back on a set date.

## The Malaysian Government Borrows By Selling Bonds Called MGS.

They're Malaysian Government Securities (MGS), long-term bonds the government sells to raise money at home for development spending. BNM sells them for the government through auctions. They pay interest twice a year. The main ones run for 3, 5, 7 and 10 years, and the government has also sold 15-year and 20-year ones.

MGS are the most actively traded bonds in Malaysia, which is why their yields are the ones people quote.

## When Yields Go Up, Bond Prices Go Down.

Each bond's interest payment is fixed when it's first sold. If interest rates rise, new bonds pay more, so an older bond paying less is worth less and its price falls. Whoever buys it at that lower price earns a higher return on their money. That return is the yield, so a rising yield and a falling bond price are the same move.

## Higher Yields Mean Bonds And Bond Funds Are Worth Less.

If you own a bond fund, its value moves the opposite way to yields: rising yields pull it down, and falling yields lift it.

Banks hold MGS too, so a jump in yields means the bonds they own are worth less, at least on paper. When the 10-year yield rises, investors may also want a higher return before they lend to companies through bonds.

## The 10-Year MGS Yield Was 3.82% On Nov 6, 2026.

Shorter MGS paid less that day: 3.39% for three years, 3.63% for five years and 3.77% for seven years. BNM's own main rate, the [Overnight Policy Rate (OPR)](/explainers/opr/), is 2.75%. BNM kept it there on Nov 5, 2026.
