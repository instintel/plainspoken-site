*Updated Nov 23, 2026*

A data centre is a building full of computer servers that store, process and send data, the machines behind the cloud services and AI (artificial intelligence) tools you use. From February 2025 to July 2026, a government task force approved 27 new ones with RM225.97 billion of planned investment, the Digital Ministry told Parliament in October 2026.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) said on Oct 15, 2026 that data centre investment has added to growth in Malaysia and other Asian economies. Growth here means [GDP](/explainers/gdp/) (gross domestic product), the measure of everything the economy produces.

## Johor Is The Centre Of The Boom.

Of the 27 projects, 16 are in Johor, 10 in Selangor and one in Negeri Sembilan. One is running and 26 are being built.

Singapore's limits on land and power push big tech firms to nearby places, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) says. Johor offers direct fibre links to Singapore.

Google said on Nov 4, 2026 that it would spend another US$15 billion, about RM61 billion, in 2027 and 2028 on new campuses in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan, beside its running one at Elmina.

## The Investment Is Big, But The Jobs Are Few.

Across the 27 projects, the Digital Ministry expects 4,053 jobs, of which 3,523 are high-skilled. Google projects its new spending will support more than 37,000 local jobs a year while it builds. Once its campuses run, it projects more than 9,000, paying on average 45% above the national [median wage](/explainers/median-wage/). The OECD says the industry creates only a limited number of jobs.

## Data Centres Use A Lot Of Electricity.

The Ministry of Energy Transition and Water Transformation told Parliament in June 2026 that data centres could use 31% of Peninsular Malaysia's electricity by 2035, up from about 7% in 2026. Both figures are projections from a government committee that plans electricity supply and tariffs.

Data centre investors must pay to upgrade the power lines they need, the trade minister said on Oct 15, 2026. The energy minister told Parliament these costs won't be unfairly passed on to other electricity users. On Nov 10, 2026, the trade minister said the connected load charge for data centres, a charge on grid power they book but don't use, rose from RM8.50 to RM89.64 per kilowatt, with no start date given.

On Oct 27, 2026, the energy minister told Parliament the government had approved a rule that data centres use at least 30% renewable energy. The Energy Commission is still drawing up its guidelines, with no start date yet.

## The Rules On AI Chips And Data Centres Are Getting Tighter.

On Nov 16, 2026, the US Commerce Department said exporters will need a US licence to ship advanced AI chips, like the ones Nvidia makes, to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. Data centres anywhere will also need one to rent large amounts of computing power to companies based in China. The rule was due to be printed in the Federal Register, the US government's official record of rules, on Nov 19, 2026, and to take effect that day, The Star and EdgeProp reported. From the day it takes effect, data centres already serving those companies have 90 days to get a licence.

Malaysia's trade minister said on Nov 18 that data centre operators here must follow the US rules, while Malaysia asks the US to clarify them. That day, the governor of Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) said it was too early to judge the rules' impact on data centres. Only 17 of the 120 data centre companies here are Chinese-owned, he said.

Since Jul 14, 2025, Malaysia has required its own permit to send US-made AI chips out of or through the country. On Nov 12, 2026, the trade minister said a law will also make data centres disclose their power use and how many AI chips they import.

Malaysia's wider data centre law, the Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill, is in final drafting, with plans to take it to Parliament in 2027, the Digital Ministry said in November 2026.
