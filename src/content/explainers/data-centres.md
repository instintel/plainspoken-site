*Updated Nov 3, 2026*

A data centre is a building full of computer servers that store, process and send data, the machines behind the cloud services and AI (artificial intelligence) tools you use. From February 2025 to July 2026, Malaysia approved 27 large new ones with RM225.97 billion of planned investment, the Digital Ministry told Parliament in October 2026.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) said on Oct 15, 2026 that data centre investment in Malaysia and other Asian economies has added to growth. Growth is measured by [GDP](/explainers/gdp/), or gross domestic product, the measure of how much the whole economy produces.

## Data Centres Do The Work Behind Apps And AI Tools.

When you back up photos, stream a show or ask an AI a question, a data centre somewhere does the work. The servers run all day and need power, and they need cooling so they don't overheat.

## Johor Is The Centre Of The Boom.

Of the 27 approved projects, 16 are in Johor, 10 are in Selangor and one is in Negeri Sembilan. One is running and 26 are being built.

Singapore's limits on land and power push big tech firms to nearby places, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) says. Johor offers fast connections and direct fibre links to Singapore. In October 2026, DayOne, a Singapore-based builder, said it had secured another RM15 billion in bank financing to expand its Johor data centres.

## The Investment Is Big, But The Jobs Are Few.

Across the 27 projects, the Digital Ministry expects 4,053 jobs, of which 3,523 are high-skilled. The OECD says the industry creates only a limited number of jobs compared with the scale of investment.

Malaysia wants these to be skilled jobs, in areas such as engineering and cybersecurity, the OECD says. It is also building up local firms to supply the software and maintenance these buildings need. Each project must have at least 30% local content.

## Data Centres Use A Lot Of Electricity.

The OECD also says the industry strains the grid and water supply in certain areas. The Ministry of Energy Transition and Water Transformation told Parliament in June 2026 that data centres use about 7% of electricity this year. Projections it cited, from a government committee that plans electricity supply and tariffs, say that could reach 31% by 2035. Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB) supplied power to 36 operating data centres in the first quarter of 2026, with another 23 under construction.

New projects are approved only once power and water supply are secured. On Oct 15, 2026, the trade minister said data centre investors must pay to upgrade the power lines they need. They must also pay for all the power they book, even if they use less. In a written reply to Parliament reported on Oct 21, the energy minister said these extra costs won't be unfairly passed on to other electricity users. Those who add the costs must bear them, he said.

Water is priced separately too. Since Aug 1, 2025, data centres in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan have paid their own water rate of RM5.30 to RM5.38 a cubic metre, and they must now recycle water. Domestic water tariffs won't be raised just to meet data centre demand, a deputy minister said in December 2025.

## Power Supply Is The Next Test.

- Every six months: each approved project reports to the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) on its local content
- 2028 to 2031: up to 9,600 megawatts (MW) of new power generation is planned to come online
