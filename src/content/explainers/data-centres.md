*Updated Nov 7, 2026*

A data centre is a building full of computer servers that store, process and send data, the machines behind the cloud services and AI (artificial intelligence) tools you use. From February 2025 to July 2026, Malaysia approved 27 large new ones with RM225.97 billion of planned investment, the Digital Ministry told Parliament in October 2026.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) said on Oct 15, 2026 that data centre investment in Malaysia and other Asian economies has added to growth. Growth here means [GDP](/explainers/gdp/), or gross domestic product, the measure of how much the whole economy produces.

## Data Centres Do The Work Behind Apps And AI Tools.

When you back up photos, stream a show or ask an AI a question, a data centre somewhere does the work. The servers run all day, and they need power and cooling so they don't overheat.

## Johor Is The Centre Of The Boom.

Of the 27 approved projects, 16 are in Johor, 10 are in Selangor and one is in Negeri Sembilan. One is running and 26 are being built.

Singapore's limits on land and power push big tech firms to nearby places, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) says. Johor offers fast connections and direct fibre links to Singapore. In October 2026, DayOne, a Singapore-based builder, said it had secured another RM15 billion in bank financing to expand its Johor data centres.

On Wednesday, Nov 4, 2026, Google said it would spend another US$15 billion, about RM61 billion, in 2027 and 2028, on top of the US$2 billion it announced in 2024. It will expand its campus at Elmina in Selangor, which is now running, and build new ones in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan.

## The Investment Is Big, But The Jobs Are Few.

Across the 27 projects, the Digital Ministry expects 4,053 jobs, of which 3,523 are high-skilled. Google projects its new spending will support more than 37,000 local jobs a year while it builds. Once its campuses run, it projects more than 9,000, paying on average 45% above the national [median wage](/explainers/median-wage/). The OECD says the industry creates only a limited number of jobs compared with the scale of investment.

Malaysia wants these to be skilled jobs, in areas such as engineering and cybersecurity, the OECD says. Each project must also have at least 30% local content, which it reports on to the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) every six months.

## Data Centres Use A Lot Of Electricity.

The OECD also says the industry strains the grid and water supply in certain areas. The Ministry of Energy Transition and Water Transformation told Parliament in June 2026 that data centres use about 7% of electricity this year. Projections it cited, from a government committee that plans electricity supply and tariffs, say that could reach 31% by 2035.

New projects are approved only once power and water supply are secured. On Oct 15, 2026, the trade minister said data centre investors must pay to upgrade the power lines they need. In a written reply to Parliament reported on Oct 21, the energy minister said these extra costs won't be unfairly passed on to other electricity users.

On Oct 27, 2026, the energy minister told Parliament the government had approved a rule that data centres use at least 30% renewable energy. The Energy Commission is still drawing up the guidelines, and no start date has been given. Google says it will add more than 1 gigawatt of clean power to the grid with Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB).

Water is priced separately too. Since Aug 1, 2025, data centres in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan have paid their own water rate of RM5.30 to RM5.38 a cubic metre. The trade minister also said on Oct 15 that data centres must now use water recycling. Domestic water tariffs won't be raised just to meet data centre demand, a deputy minister said in December 2025.

## A Draft Data Centre Law Is Planned For 2027.

In 2027, the government plans to send a draft data centre law, the Digital Infrastructure (Data Centre) Bill, to the Cabinet.
