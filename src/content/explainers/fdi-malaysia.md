*Updated Nov 11, 2026*

Foreign direct investment (FDI) is money foreign companies put into businesses in Malaysia to own at least 10% of them for the long run, and Malaysia's Finance Minister II said in May 2026 that it has started adding to economic growth. In 2025 it came to RM65.9 billion, up 41.2% from RM46.7 billion in 2024, the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) says.

## DOSM And Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) Count FDI Every Quarter, Once The Money Has Arrived.

Every quarter, DOSM and BNM survey companies. FDI counts only money that actually came in during that quarter. It covers money for new shares, profits a foreign owner keeps in the business here, and loans between the business and its foreign owner.

Shares and bonds, which are loans to a company or government, are counted separately when they are bought for the short term, as portfolio investment.

FDI is also different from the approved investment the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) announces. MIDA counts projects it has approved, which it calls potential investment. It says projects usually take 18 to 24 months to get going after approval. Google's plan, announced on Nov 4, 2026, to invest US$15 billion here in 2027 and 2028 is a pledge too, while DOSM's FDI counts money that has already come in.

## Singapore, Hong Kong And China Sent The Most In 2025.

Each investment is filed under the country the money came from directly. In 2025, Singapore, Hong Kong and China were the top three.

Most of the money, RM59.5 billion of the RM65.9 billion, went into services. Of that, 53.6% went into information and communication. DOSM said that share reflects the global shift to digital and the expansion of [data centre](/explainers/data-centres/) infrastructure.

## Factory Projects That Went Ahead, Mostly Foreign-Funded, Created 210,546 Jobs.

In July 2026, the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) told Parliament that 2,688 factory projects approved from 2023 to March 2026 had actually gone ahead. They are worth RM318.5 billion, 80.1% of it from foreign investors. Together they created 210,546 new jobs across the country.

## FDI Came To RM7.4 Billion In April To June 2026, On DOSM's First Count.

The latest quarter DOSM has published is April to June 2026, when FDI came to RM7.4 billion, down from RM22.8 billion in January to March. Both are preliminary figures, which DOSM can still revise.

Over a full year, 2025's RM65.9 billion was the second highest of the past 10 years, behind RM75.4 billion in 2022. The lowest was RM13.3 billion in 2020.

Past years get revised too. In June 2025, DOSM put 2024 at RM51.5 billion, and it now puts that year at RM46.7 billion.

## The Next FDI Figure Comes Out On Wednesday, Nov 18, 2026.

Each quarter's FDI comes out in DOSM's balance of payments, its record of money moving between Malaysia and the rest of the world. It lands on the same day as the figures for Malaysia's gross domestic product ([GDP](/explainers/gdp/)), the value of everything the country produces.
