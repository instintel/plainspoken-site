*Updated Nov 17, 2026*

Foreign fund flows on Bursa Malaysia, the country's stock exchange, are the value of shares foreign investors buy there, minus the value they sell. In the week to Nov 13, 2026, foreign institutions sold a net RM270.2 million, after two weeks of net buying.

Local retail investors, people trading their own money, bought a net RM326.3 million that week, their 12th straight week of buying. If you trade shares yourself, you're in that retail group.

## Investment Banks Count The Flows Every Week.

MBSB Investment Bank publishes a weekly fund flow report that splits Bursa trading into three groups: foreign institutions, local institutions and local retail investors. The report usually comes out on Monday, covering the week to the Friday before. It also gives each group's flows by sector, such as technology or construction.

CIMB Securities publishes its own weekly count, which splits investors into more groups, so its figures don't always match.

The groups come from Bursa Malaysia's own trading data, and Bursa changed how it sorts them from Apr 6, 2026, after announcing the change on Feb 24, 2026. A trade made through a nominee account, one held in another name for the real owner, now counts as retail or institutional depending on who really owns the shares. A Malaysian-registered company owned by foreigners is now sorted by where its money comes from, not counted as foreign by default.

## Net Selling Means Foreign Funds Sold More Than They Bought.

Net is the buying minus the selling. So in the week to Nov 13, foreign funds bought shares too, just RM270.2 million less than they sold.

Every share sold has a buyer, so one group's net selling shows up as another group's net buying. That week, local institutions sold a net RM56 million too, and local retail investors were the net buyers. Foreign funds sold the most in technology shares, a net RM241.5 million.

## Foreign Funds Sold More Than Five Times As Much In 2025 As In 2024.

In 2025, foreign funds sold a net RM22.3 billion, up from RM4.2 billion in 2024, MBSB Investment Bank's count shows. The same count puts local institutions on the other side, buying a net RM22.2 billion in 2025, with local retail investors buying about RM160 million.

## One Analyst Is Watching US Bond Yields And The Dollar.

On Nov 11, 2026, investment strategist Mohd Sedek Jantan said higher US [bond yields](/explainers/bond-yield/) and a stronger US dollar could weigh on Malaysian shares. US bond yields are basically the yearly interest the US government pays on money it borrows. He said higher yields and a stronger dollar may also limit foreign money coming into Malaysian markets. The main US interest rate is set by the [US Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/).
