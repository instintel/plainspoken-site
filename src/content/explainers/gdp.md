*Updated Nov 20, 2026*

When the news says Malaysia's economy grew 5.5% from July to September 2026, the thing being measured is GDP: the total value of the goods and services produced in Malaysia. GDP stands for gross domestic product, and 5.5% means it was that much bigger than in the same three months a year earlier, with rising prices taken out. That's slower than the 6.0% from April to June.

In Malay it's called KDNK, short for Keluaran Dalam Negara Kasar. GDP is the widest view of how busy Malaysia's businesses are.

## The Department Of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) Measures It Every Three Months.

DOSM adds up what farms, mines, factories, builders and service businesses like shops, telcos and banks produce inside Malaysia. For growth, it uses what's called real GDP, measured at 2015 prices. That way, things simply costing more doesn't get counted as the economy growing.

## Each Quarter Gets An Early Figure First, Then The Actual One.

About two weeks after a quarter ends, DOSM puts out an advance estimate, an early first figure, put out so people can see how the economy is doing sooner. It has done this since the third quarter of 2023. About a month after that comes the actual figure, which can come out a little different.

It happened in all three quarters so far this year. January to March 2026 went from 5.3% to 5.4%, April to June from 5.8% to 6.0%, and July to September from 5.3% to 5.5%, in the actual figure released on Nov 18, 2026.

## Factories Grew 7.7% From July To September, While Mining Shrank.

The breakdown shows which kinds of work are growing. From July to September 2026, manufacturing grew 7.7%, with electrical, electronic and optical products up 16.5%. Construction grew 5.9%, and services, which covers shops, telcos and banks, gave growth the most support, DOSM said. Mining shrank 6.1%, with crude oil and condensate output down 12.4%.

## Growth So Far In 2026 Is Running Above The Government's Own Range.

DOSM's 5.5% for July to September came out on Nov 18, 2026. Before that, growth was 6.2% from October to December 2025, 5.4% from January to March 2026 and 6.0% from April to June.

Over the first nine months of 2026, the economy grew 5.6%, up from 4.8% over the same months of 2025.

The Ministry of Finance (MOF) publishes a forecast for GDP alongside each Budget. It expects 4.8% to 5.3% growth for the whole of 2026, up from the 4.0% to 4.5% it expected when it drew up Budget 2026. So growth so far is running above the top of that range.
