*Updated Nov 20, 2026*

An IPO, or initial public offering, is when a company sells its shares to the public for the first time and lists them on a stock exchange, where they can then be bought and sold. From January to September 2026, Bursa Malaysia, the country's stock exchange, had 47 IPOs, against 60 in the whole of 2025.

A share is a small piece of ownership in a company, so if you get IPO shares, you become a part-owner. When an IPO includes shares for the Malaysian public, any Malaysian aged 18 and over can apply through their bank, and a ballot decides who gets them if too many people apply.

## The Securities Commission Approves Main Market IPOs, And Bursa Approves ACE Market Ones.

Bursa has three markets for shares. The Main Market is for larger, more established companies. The ACE Market is for small and mid-sized ones, a stepping stone to the Main Market, says the Securities Commission Malaysia (SC), the regulator that oversees Malaysia's stock market. Then there's the LEAP Market for early-stage companies, which for now only sophisticated investors can put money into, not the general public.

A company listing on the Main Market needs the SC's approval. For the ACE Market, Bursa has been the only body approving IPOs since Jan 1, 2022. The prospectus, the company's official document for the offer, is registered by the SC for a Main Market IPO and by Bursa for an ACE Market one.

## At Least A Quarter Of A Listed Company's Shares Must Usually Be In Public Hands.

Bursa's listing rules put the minimum at 25% of the shares. Companies worth RM1 billion or more can ask Bursa for a lower figure, down to 15% for those worth RM3 billion or more.

Main Market companies whose business is mainly in Malaysia also set aside 12.5% of their shares at listing for Bumiputera investors. Those investors are approved or recognised by the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI). And when these companies offer shares to the Malaysian public by ballot, at least half of the balloted shares go to Bumiputera applicants, the SC says.

## Malaysians Apply Through A Bank, And A Ballot Decides Who Gets Shares.

Applications are made through participating banks' internet banking or ATMs, or on a paper form, IPO prospectuses say. Each applicant needs a Central Depository System (CDS) account in their own name. That's the account that holds shares bought on Bursa. Each person can apply only once in each category of the offer, for at least 100 shares or a multiple of 100.

If more people apply than there are shares, the issuing house, the company handling the applications, runs a ballot. It posts the results on its website within one market day (a day Bursa is open) of the ballot, and money from applications that miss out is refunded without interest. Successful applicants get the shares in their CDS accounts, and trading on Bursa starts from the company's listing day.

## Bursa Had 60 IPOs In 2025, Twice As Many As In 2021.

In 2025, Bursa had 60 IPOs: 11 on the Main Market, 44 on the ACE Market and five on the LEAP Market. That was up from 55 in 2024 and 30 in 2021.

The 47 IPOs from January to September 2026 raised RM6.1 billion between them, Bursa reported on Oct 28, 2026.

## Main Market Rules Got Stricter On June 3, 2026, And More Changes Are Pending.

Since June 3, 2026, a company listing on the Main Market through the profit test needs at least RM30 million of after-tax profit over its last three full financial years, up from RM20 million. Its latest year needs at least RM15 million, up from RM6 million. The SC announced this on May 28, 2026, along with changes for the ACE Market, including a minimum amount of shares set aside for the public, with no start date given.

In May 2026, the SC and Bursa also proposed letting ordinary retail investors into the LEAP Market for the first time, up to RM250,000 each at any one time. Feedback closed on June 15, 2026, and no start date has been set.
