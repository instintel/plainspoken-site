*Updated Oct 18, 2026*

The FBM KLCI is Malaysia's main share index: one number that follows the share prices of the 30 biggest companies on Bursa Malaysia, the country's stock exchange. When the news says the KLCI fell 0.98%, it means those 30 companies' shares, taken together, lost 0.98% of their value that day.

Its full name is the FTSE Bursa Malaysia KLCI. The number of companies is fixed, so there are always exactly 30.

Moves are given in points and in percent. The percent is the one to look at, because it lets you compare days: on Oct 16, 2026, a fall of 15.94 points was 0.98%.

## The 30 companies are picked by size, twice a year.

The index follows FTSE Russell's rules for the FTSE Bursa Malaysia index series. It holds the 30 largest companies on Bursa Malaysia's Main Market by market value, which is a company's share price times its number of shares. The list is reviewed every June and December, so a company that grows can move in and one that shrinks can drop out.

## Bigger companies move the index more.

Each company counts in proportion to its size, but only its free float counts: the shares that are available for anyone to buy and sell, leaving out large blocks held by long-term owners. A company with 15% or less of its shares free to trade can't get in at all. So a 5% jump in one of the largest companies moves the KLCI far more than a 5% jump in the 30th.

## What it means for your money.

If you own Malaysian shares, or a unit trust that holds them, the KLCI is a quick read on how the biggest of them did that day. It covers only 30 companies, though, so your own shares can go the other way.

It's also a quick read on mood. On Oct 16, 2026, the KLCI fell nearly 1% on the same day Malaysia's growth figure came in better than many forecasters expected, which shows the index doesn't always follow the economy day to day.

## The KLCI ended Oct 16, 2026 at 1,607.76.

That was 15.94 points, or 0.98%, lower than the day before.
