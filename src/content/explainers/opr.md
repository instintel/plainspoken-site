*Updated Nov 6, 2026*

The OPR, short for Overnight Policy Rate, is the main interest rate Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) sets, and it shapes what borrowing costs across Malaysia. BNM kept it at 2.75% on Nov 5, 2026, where it has sat since a cut from 3.00% in July 2025. When BNM moves it, banks change the rate on floating-rate loans to match, so your monthly instalment on that kind of loan goes up or down with it.

An interest rate is basically the price of borrowing money: the extra you pay back on top of what you borrowed.

## A Committee At BNM Decides The OPR, At Least Six Times A Year.

That group is BNM's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC). It's made up of the Governor, the Deputy Governors and a few other members, including some from outside the central bank. The MPC looks at how fast prices are rising and how fast the economy is growing, then decides where the OPR should sit. After every meeting it publishes a short statement explaining what it decided and why.

## It Starts With Banks Lending To Each Other Overnight.

Every day some banks finish with spare cash while others come up short, so they lend to each other for one night. BNM manages how much cash is moving around so that the rate on those overnight loans stays close to the OPR. That makes the OPR the base cost of money for banks, which is why BNM uses it to steer the other interest rates in the economy, including the ones on your loans.

## If Your Loan Is Floating, The OPR Is Built Into It.

Since Aug 1, 2022, new floating-rate loans to retail customers like you, and loans refinanced after that date, are priced off something called the Standardised Base Rate (SBR). BNM tied the SBR to the OPR alone, so the SBR only changes when the OPR changes. Your bank then adds its own margin on top, for things like its costs and how risky it thinks lending to you is.

Older floating-rate loans still run on the bank's Base Rate or Base Lending Rate. Since Aug 2022, those move exactly in step with the SBR too. So once the OPR moves, the rate on a floating-rate loan follows, and so does its monthly instalment. Since July 1, 2026, BNM's rules require your bank to change that instalment within 60 days of your loan's reference rate moving, and from Jan 2, 2028, within 30 days.

A fixed-rate loan works differently. Its rate is agreed upfront for the whole loan, so the instalment stays the same whatever happens to the OPR.

Savings work differently too. What a bank pays on your savings account or fixed deposit is up to each bank and each product, so it doesn't move automatically with the OPR, or by the same amount.

## The OPR Has Been 2.75% Since July 2025.

BNM cut the OPR from 3.00% to 2.75% on July 9, 2025. It has kept it at 2.75% at all eight meetings since, the latest on Nov 5, 2026, its last meeting of the year. So 2.75% is the OPR as at Nov 6, 2026.

Before that cut, the OPR sat at 3.00% for more than two years. BNM got it there from 1.75% with five rises of 0.25 percentage point each, four in 2022 and the last on May 3, 2023.
