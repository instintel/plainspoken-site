*Updated Nov 3, 2026*

The ringgit's value is how many ringgit it takes to buy another currency, most often the US dollar, so it moves what you pay for anything priced in one. On Nov 2, 2026, one US dollar cost about RM4.07, down from about RM4.19 at the end of October 2025. When that number goes down, the ringgit is stronger and anything priced in US dollars costs you less. When it goes up, it costs you more.

That number is called the exchange rate.

## The Market Sets The Ringgit's Price, And The Central Bank Steps In Only To Keep Trading Orderly.

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM), Malaysia's central bank, says the ringgit has a flexible exchange rate. Its value is set by banks, businesses and investors buying and selling it every day, and BNM doesn't aim for any one level. BNM says it only trades in the market to stop sudden or large swings and to keep trading orderly.

## The Ringgit Gets Stronger When More People Want To Buy It.

Every day, some people want ringgit and some want to swap their ringgit for something else. When more of them want to buy ringgit, its price goes up and it gets stronger. When more of them want US dollars, the ringgit gets weaker.

Interest rates are one of the things that shift this. When the [US Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/), the US central bank, raises its rate, holding US dollars pays more. That tends to make the US dollar stronger against the ringgit. BNM lists other things that move the ringgit too, such as how fast prices rise in Malaysia compared with other countries, and how the world economy is doing.

## Anything Priced In Another Currency Moves With The Ringgit.

The exchange rate reaches you in four ways:

- Goods Malaysia buys from overseas, food included, cost more in ringgit when the ringgit is weaker, and less when it's stronger
- Holidays overseas, studying abroad and money you send overseas all change in ringgit terms
- A loan or bill in US dollars takes more ringgit to pay when the ringgit weakens
- If your business sells overseas, a stronger ringgit turns each US dollar you earn into fewer ringgit

That last one works the other way too. BNM says a weaker ringgit is good for a business that depends on exports.

## The Ringgit Was About RM4.07 To The US Dollar On Nov 2, 2026.

On Nov 2, 2026, the ringgit ended at 4.0695 to the US dollar, a little weaker than 4.0555 at the end of October. So something priced at US$100, like an online course or a part your business imports, cost about RM407, against about RM419 at the end of October 2025.

Over that year the ringgit moved both ways. One US dollar cost 4.1860 ringgit at the end of October 2025, and only 3.8910 at the end of February 2026, the lowest of the 13 month-end closes in the chart below.
