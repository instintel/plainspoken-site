*Updated Nov 12, 2026*

Malaysia's unemployment rate is the share of people working or looking for work who have no job, as counted by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM). In September 2026 it was 2.9%, about 3 in every 100: 513,200 people without work, against 16.89 million with jobs.

If you're 15 to 24, the number is a lot higher. In August 2026, 10.1% of young people working or looking for work had no job, about one in 10, DOSM's figures show.

## DOSM Asks Households About One Week Of Work, Every Month.

DOSM runs the Labour Force Survey. It goes to households and asks what each working-age person, aged 15 to 64, did during one set week, called the reference week. The survey follows the guidelines of the International Labour Organization (ILO).

DOSM publishes the rate every month, a fuller report every quarter and a report for the whole year. Past months can change: DOSM revised its December 2025 figures when it updated its population estimates, it said in March 2026.

## You Count As Employed If You Worked Just One Hour That Week.

Anyone who worked at least one hour for pay, profit or family gain during the reference week counts as employed. A side hustle counts. So does helping in the family business without pay.

You count as unemployed if you did no work at all that week and were ready to work and looking for a job, or weren't looking for reasons such as believing no work was available or that you weren't qualified. DOSM calls these two groups the actively and inactively unemployed. In July to September 2026, 79.7% of unemployed people were actively looking.

Everyone else is outside the labour force and left out of the rate completely. In 2025 that was 7.03 million people, mostly people doing housework or looking after family, and students or people in training.

## The Rate Can Look Low While People Still Want Better Work.

Because one hour of work counts as a job, the rate doesn't show whether you have enough hours or a job that fits your studies. DOSM counts those separately, as underemployment: people working under 30 hours a week who want more hours, and people with tertiary education, meaning college or university, working in semi-skilled or low-skilled jobs.

## The Rate Was 2.9% In September 2026, The Same As August.

That is the latest monthly reading, published on Nov 11, 2026. For the whole of 2025 the rate was 3.0%, the lowest in a decade, DOSM said, down from 3.2% in 2024. By age, it was 1.6% that year for people aged 25 to 64, against 9.6% for those aged 15 to 24.

The rate was far higher in the pandemic years: 4.5% in 2020, the highest in almost three decades, DOSM said at the time, and 4.6% in 2021.
