*Updated Oct 30, 2026*

US inflation is how fast prices in the US rise over a year. The US Federal Reserve (the Fed) aims to keep it at 2% on a measure called the PCE price index, which rose 3.6% in the year to September 2026.

## The US Has Two Main Inflation Figures, CPI And PCE.

The consumer price index (CPI) comes from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). It tracks what households pay out of their own pockets, and the main version covers people in towns and cities, more than 90% of the US population.

The PCE price index comes from the US Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA). It counts spending by households and spending made on their behalf, including by charities and other nonprofits that serve households. BEA works it out with a different formula and gives different weights to the same goods and services, so the two figures rarely match.

In September 2026, the CPI was 3.7% higher than a year earlier, and the PCE price index was 3.6% higher.

## The Fed Measures Its 2% Goal On PCE, Not CPI.

In its statement of long-run goals, the Fed says inflation of 2%, measured by the yearly change in the PCE price index, best fits its job of keeping prices stable and jobs plentiful. So the PCE figure is the one that tells you how far the Fed is from its goal.

## US Prices Reach Your Money Through The Fed And The Ringgit.

Changes to the Fed's rate feed into other interest rates and into exchange rates between currencies, the Fed says. That is the path to your wallet: a Fed move can change how many ringgit each US dollar costs, which matters for anything priced in US dollars. Our [Federal Reserve](/explainers/federal-reserve/) and [ringgit](/explainers/ringgit/) pages walk through that chain.

A US inflation figure on its own does not move the ringgit in a set direction, because the ringgit also follows Malaysia's own economy. Malaysia's own [inflation](/explainers/inflation/) is measured separately, by Malaysia's statistics department.

## US PCE Inflation Has Been Above The Fed's 2% Goal Every Month Since November 2025.

PCE inflation was 2.8% in November 2025 and still 2.8% in February 2026. It then rose to 3.5% in March, peaked at 4.1% in May, and was 3.6% in September, up from 3.4% in August. The CPI took a similar path, peaking at 4.2% in May. These are BEA's figures as first published each month; BEA revised its earlier numbers back to 2021 in a yearly update on Sep 30, 2026.
