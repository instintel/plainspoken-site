*2-minute read*

Malaysians aged 30 and below made 65.9 million buy now, pay later (BNPL) purchases in the first half of this year, worth RM5.4 billion, the Ministry of Finance (MOF) told Parliament last month.

![Photo of three cardboard parcels on a doormat outside a front door.](/images/trending/2026-11-11/photo-buy-now-pay-later-2026-11-11.jpg)

BNPL lets you take something now and pay for it in instalments, and it's credit, like any loan. So if you're paying later on anything in today's 11.11 sales, a new law has covered it since March.

Under the Consumer Credit Act 2025, BNPL companies answer to a new regulator, the Consumer Credit Commission (SKP). Before approving you, a provider has to check that you can afford the repayments, and it can't give or raise credit for someone likely to struggle with them. It also has to clearly state its terms, fees and charges.

On average, each purchase comes to roughly RM80. Young users mainly used BNPL for groceries, food, transport and services last year, Deputy Finance Minister Liew Chin Tong told Parliament in January.

Young people make up almost 40% of all BNPL purchases in Malaysia, and the number of purchases rose 23.9% in the first half of 2026 from the six months before.

Young users still owed RM2.4 billion on BNPL at the end of June. RM96.8 million of that, or 4.0%, was non-performing, the label for repayments that have fallen seriously behind.

BNPL companies have six months from June 1 to apply for an SKP licence, a window that closes around the start of December.
