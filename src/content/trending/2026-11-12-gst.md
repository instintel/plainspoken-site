*1-minute read*

Malaysians' minimum income has to reach RM4,000 before goods and services tax (GST) can be considered again, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told Parliament on Thursday.

![Photo of a long row of empty metal supermarket trolleys lined up against a wall.](/images/trending/2026-11-12/photo-gst-2026-11-12.jpg)

For now, he said, Malaysia stays with the sales and service tax (SST).

In the yearly pay survey by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), no age group had [median](/explainers/median-wage/) pay as high as RM4,000 in 2025. The median is the middle point, so half earn less. For employees aged 25 to 29 it was RM2,285 a month. Even the best-paid age group, 40 to 44, was at RM3,826.

GST is a broad tax that applies whatever you earn, which is why Anwar, who is also Finance Minister, says it would weigh on lower-income earners. Malaysia charged it at 6% from April 2015 until it was cut to zero in June 2018, and SST replaced it that September.

SST is mainly a tax on businesses, Anwar said, though a small part gets passed on to shoppers. "I choose to tax businesses instead," he said.

The government is open to fixing parts of SST, he added, including cases where businesses get taxed twice on the same thing.
