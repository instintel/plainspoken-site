A cyberattack on a bank won't make your loan disappear, two academics said in a Bernama report on Friday. One of them, Prof Zubaile Abdullah of Universiti Tun Hussein Onn Malaysia (UTHM), said he had seen comments online hoping hackers would hit the banks and wipe out people's debts.

![Photo of an open combination padlock standing on a laptop keyboard.](/images/trending/2026-11-13/photo-bank-cyberattack-2026-11-13.jpg)

Loan records may sit across several systems, Zubaile said, with backups and transaction histories behind them, and the bank still has your loan agreement. So one deleted record doesn't mean the debt is gone.

An attack can still cause real trouble, he warned. It can change or lock a bank's data for a while, and mess up balances and payments.

The talk follows Bank Rakyat's corporate website going down on the night of Nov 6. The bank blamed a technical problem at an outside provider, and said customers' deposits and personal data weren't affected.

On Thursday, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said the National Cyber Security Agency (NACSA) hasn't confirmed any of the attack claims going around on social media. Its daily reports show no worrying incidents so far, he said. If anything is confirmed, he said, NACSA will be the one to say so.
