*2-minute read*

Nvidia lost just under US$600 billion in stock market value in the week Jensen Huang quit as its chief executive, AP reported. He co-founded it 33 years ago.

![Photo of a computer chip with rows of gold pins, lying on a plain grey surface.](/images/trending/2026-11-14/photo-jensen-huang-2026-11-14.jpg)

The company is the biggest maker of the chips that run AI. On Friday, Bursa Malaysia's main share index, the KLCI, fell for a fifth day running, and a Rakuten Trade analyst said investors were nervous, partly over uncertainty about Nvidia's leadership.

Nvidia gave no reason for his exit. Days earlier, Huang emailed staff: "I was unfaithful to my wife." The New York Times reported an affair with a businesswoman US intelligence suspects of spying for China, which Beijing denies, and one with an executive who reported to him. Nvidia said it had "no reason to believe" national security or its secrets were compromised.

That businesswoman is a former executive at a Singapore [data-centre](/explainers/data-centres/) company. The Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) is checking whether AI chips that company and a linked Malaysian company brought here were sent abroad. Bringing AI chips in needs no permit; sending powerful US ones on has needed a MITI permit since July 2025.

Finance chief Colette Kress is running Nvidia for now. Its results for the latest three months come out at about 5:20am on Wednesday, Nov 18, Malaysia time.

An analyst at Saxo Markets says Nvidia's tech lead won't vanish with Huang, but warns a sudden exit without a credible succession plan could make investors rethink how much depends on him. After this week, how much of Nvidia rests on one person? Huang pushed for looser US limits on chip sales to China; with US lawmakers now asking about the spy claims, who pushes next? And if MITI finds chips left without that permit, does bringing them in stay permit-free?
