The Employees Provident Fund (EPF) made RM83.32 billion from investing members' savings from January to September, 30% more than a year earlier, but it says the growth is already slowing. The nine-month total beats the RM79.2 billion it made from investing in all of 2025.

![Photo of cupped hands holding soil with a small green seedling, in light rain.](/images/trending/2026-11-17/photo-epf-2026-11-17.jpg)

If you have an EPF account, this is your retirement money at work. EPF has over 18.6 million members, and it invests their savings in shares, bonds and property, in Malaysia and abroad.

The slowdown shows in the latest quarter. From July to September, EPF made RM25.82 billion, just 3% more than a year before, after 44% growth from April to June, The Edge reported.

"We saw a deceleration of income growth in the third quarter, a trend which we expect to continue in the fourth quarter," chief executive Ahmad Zulqarnain Onn said. He pointed to weak global stock markets and rising bond yields, which push down the value of the bonds EPF holds.
