*2-minute read*

Malaysia's economy grew 5.5% from July to September, beating the 5.3% first estimate, which the Economy Minister said only on Tuesday he expected to stay, or come in "a bit better".

![Photo taken from above of a cargo ship loaded with containers beside a port, with tugboats alongside.](/images/trending/2026-11-18/photo-malaysia-gdp-2026-11-18.jpg)

That's the official count of [GDP](/explainers/gdp/), the value of everything produced in Malaysia, from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) and Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM), measured against the same months last year. It's slower than the 6.0% from April to June, but growth for the first nine months still comes to 5.6%.

BNM says household spending was held up by a good job market and steady income growth. Spending still grew more slowly than before, it says, because cash handouts were bigger in the same months of 2025.

Exports, what Malaysia sells to other countries, helped too. BNM says they grew faster, led by tech products. September's exports hit a record RM215.1 billion, the first month above RM200 billion, Bernama reported.

The part to watch is prices. [Inflation](/explainers/inflation/), how fast prices rise, was 1.9% in those three months. BNM expects it to edge up towards the end of the year and into early 2027, as higher costs for businesses pass through to prices with a delay. It says fuel subsidies and steady spending should keep that in check.

BNM Governor Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour expects growth above 5% for the whole year. On new US limits on advanced AI chips for data centres here, he said it's too early to judge the impact. He expects data centre investment to stay strong until at least 2028.
