*2-minute read*

Malaysia's biggest banks must be ready to share your account information with other financial firms by January 1, 2028, if you agree, under rules Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) set on Tuesday. BNM calls it open finance, and says it "gives customers greater control over how their financial information is shared."

![Photo of two hands holding two smartphones edge to edge, with takeaway coffee cups blurred in the background.](/images/trending/2026-11-18/photo-open-finance-2026-11-18.jpg)

If you have a bank account or an e-wallet, or carry a credit card, this is your own money trail: the balance and the last 12 months of transactions on each one.

The biggest banks go first: those with more than one million active individual and small business customers. Smaller banks with more than 250,000 such customers, and e-wallet companies with more than five million active users, join by January 1, 2029, with more lenders following in 2030.

Nothing moves without a clear yes. Pre-ticked boxes are banned, consent can't be bundled into other terms, and you can withdraw it at any time from a dashboard each firm must give you. Ongoing access lasts six months at most before you have to renew it, and the firm can use your data only for the purpose you agreed to, unless the law allows otherwise.

Saying no can't be held against you either, unless it makes a real difference to whether you qualify for the product. Any fee for open finance must be fair and transparent, BNM says.
