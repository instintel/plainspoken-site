*2-minute read*

Relief that spares about one million more homes three charges on their electricity bill ends on Dec 31, and the government hasn't decided whether to extend it.

![Photo of a ceiling fan turning, seen from below, its blades blurred against a white ceiling.](/images/trending/2026-11-19/photo-tnb-bill-2026-11-19.jpg)

Since September, homes using up to 800 kWh of electricity a month have paid no fuel surcharge, no RM10 retail charge and no 8% service tax. That's a bill of about RM328 or less. The limit used to be 600 kWh, and raising it brought in about one million more homes, Tenaga Nasional Bhd (TNB) said.

If the limit goes back to 600 kWh on Jan 1, homes using more than that pay those charges again. The fuel surcharge, called the automatic fuel adjustment (AFA), is 3.60 sen per kWh in November. Single Buyer, which works it out, projects 6.23 sen for January, but the Energy Commission sets the actual rate each month.

Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof, also the energy minister, said in a written reply to Parliament that any decision on extending or widening the relief will come after the surcharge review.

That review is looking at sharing costs more fairly from 2027, with users paying for the costs they cause. On Wednesday, his deputy Abdul Rahman Mohamad told Parliament the proposal for a surcharge in tiers hasn't been finalised.

The four months of relief are estimated to cost RM248 million, and TNB is carrying most of it.
