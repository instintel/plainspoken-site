Nvidia's board has brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive, 11 days after [he quit](/trending/2026-11-14-jensen-huang/) and six days after it said it did not intend to.

![Close-up of a green printed circuit board, with thin gold tracks running between rows of small round solder points.](/images/trending/2026-11-23/photo-jensen-huang-returns-2026-11-23.jpg)

Nvidia is the biggest maker of the chips that run AI, and its shares jumped about 30% before the US market opened on Monday, AFP reported. On Friday, they had fallen 14%, ending a week down more than 20%.

The board voted unanimously, saying it was satisfied with his candour and his ability to lead. It also said it received input from senior levels of the US government and is confident his return poses no national security risk.

Huang quit on Nov 12 after reports of affairs, including one with a businesswoman US officials suspected of spying for China, which Beijing denies. A board committee looking into reports that he was the target of a foreign intelligence effort will see its investigation through, Nvidia said, and Huang will cooperate. Nvidia has also set up a board committee on national security and export controls, and is looking for a new chief compliance officer.

Colette Kress, who ran Nvidia while he was gone, stays on as finance chief.

"I can't wait to get back to work," Huang said.

## What happens next

- **Tuesday, Dec 1:** Huang is due to answer questions from US House committees in a closed-door interview. Nvidia's top lawyer is due the day after.
