*2-minute read*

Medical insurance payouts in Malaysia, the money insurers pay on claims, have grown more than 13-fold since 2003, Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) Governor Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour said on Saturday night. Back then, they were under RM1 billion.

![An empty hospital bed with its rails raised in a quiet private room, with patterned curtains, a chair and a vase of flowers by a tall window.](/images/trending/2026-11-23/photo-medical-insurance-2026-11-23.jpg)

"Medical costs are rising. Claims are escalating. And premiums are following," he told a medical alumni dinner. Premiums are what you pay to keep your medical card. For some families, he said, keeping meaningful cover is getting harder.

His point: holding premiums down without tackling what care costs only shifts the pressure elsewhere. Private spending on healthcare, including insurance and what people pay out of pocket, is now almost on par with public spending, he said.

One of BNM's fixes is MediAsas, a basic medical insurance and takaful (Islamic insurance) plan due to launch in January, he said. It pays up to RM100,000 a year, or RM150,000 for people over 60. The authorities will set its premiums, with final prices announced closer to the launch.

If you already have a medical card, BNM says you will be able to switch to MediAsas with the same insurer or takaful operator. You won't need a fresh medical assessment or a new waiting period. Meanwhile, BNM's limit on yearly premium rises from higher medical claims, no more than 10% for most policyholders, runs to the end of 2026.

Parliament's Public Accounts Committee wants MediAsas's small trial run widened before launch. Its chairman said no one in the trial was admitted to hospital, The Star reported. The governor said on Nov 18 that the trial was meant to test how the system runs.

## What happens next

- **By the end of January 2027:** MediAsas is due to roll out nationwide, the Deputy Finance Minister told Parliament on Nov 11.
