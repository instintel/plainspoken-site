*2-minute read*

Since 2024, 404,848 Malaysians have been put under travel restrictions that can stop them leaving the country, Immigration Department director-general Zakaria Shaaban said in an interview reported by The Star.

![Rows of empty black seats in a bright airport waiting area, with large windows behind them.](/images/trending/2026-11-23/photo-travel-ban-2026-11-23.jpg)

The reasons he listed include unpaid income tax, unpaid loans from the National Higher Education Fund Corporation (PTPTN) and outstanding Employees Provident Fund (EPF) contributions or other EPF obligations. Customs matters, [bankruptcy](/explainers/bankruptcy-malaysia/) cases and court orders are among them too. One person can be on the list for more than one agency.

If you're stopped at the border, paying there won't lift it on the spot. Immigration doesn't collect money for other agencies, he said. You have to settle it with the agency that put the restriction on you, and your status changes only once that agency tells Immigration.

You can check before you travel. Immigration's online checker, Sistem Semakan Status Perjalanan Imigresen (SSPI), asks for your 12-digit IC number. For tax, the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN) says its online tax portal, MyTax, shows it too.

LHDN lifts a tax restriction once you pay in full, its website says. If you can't, you can ask for a temporary release by paying 50% of the tax you owe. You apply at the LHDN office handling your file, with your receipt and a letter saying where you're going, why and for how long, five days before you travel.
