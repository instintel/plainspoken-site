Graduates are questioning whether a degree still pays off, theSun reported on Tuesday. A Threads post said a first job pays only RM1,700 to RM3,000, and it takes years to pay off the education loan, on top of rent and living costs.

![Rows of graduates in caps and gowns seen from behind at a graduation ceremony, in black and white.](/images/trending/2026-11-24/photo-graduate-salary-2026-11-24.jpg)

Graduates with a degree earned a [median](/explainers/median-wage/) RM5,724 a month in 2024, and diploma holders RM3,390, the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) said. The median is the middle: half earn more, half less. Those figures cover all graduates, not just new ones.

DOSM's figures don't say what new graduates start on. RM1,700 is the legal [minimum wage](/explainers/minimum-wage/), the least any private employer must pay you.

DOSM also found 32.2% of working graduates in jobs below their qualifications in 2024, about 1.6 million people. Graduate unemployment was 3.2%.

As of Aug 31, 1.16 million borrowers owed RM10.7 billion in outstanding repayments to the National Higher Education Fund Corporation (PTPTN). The Higher Education Minister gave the figure in a written reply to Parliament in October.

If you're behind on yours, PTPTN's Borrower-Friendly Month runs until Dec 15. You can restructure or consolidate your loan by paying as little as 20% of your arrears or RM1,000, whichever is higher, at a PTPTN counter or through its other service channels.

## What happens next
