*2-minute read*

Malaysia's [data centres](/explainers/data-centres/) will be told not to host or serve seven firms the US has just blacklisted, the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) said on Tuesday.

![An aisle between rows of dark server racks with blue lights, under a white tiled ceiling in a data centre.](/images/trending/2026-11-24/photo-us-blacklist-2026-11-24.jpg)

The US Commerce Department added the seven, in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, to its trade blacklist, the Entity List, on Monday, US time. It cited a significant risk that they could pass advanced computer chips to users it hasn't approved. Two of them are Malaysian companies, MITI said.

One is a Singapore cloud company at the centre of the Nvidia probe, and the other six all have some link to it, a Commerce official said, Reuters reported. One of the Malaysian firms is its wholly owned subsidiary, MITI said.

For these seven, anyone sending them anything covered by US export rules now needs a US licence, and each application starts from a presumption that it will be refused.

Nvidia is the biggest maker of the chips that run AI. Its boss, Jensen Huang, quit on Nov 12 after reports of an affair with a former executive of that Singapore company, whom US officials suspect of spying for China. China has called the spying accusations groundless. Nvidia [reinstated him](/fintrending/2026-11-23-jensen-huang-returns/) on Monday, the day of the listing.

The Singapore company said last week that it does legitimate business and follows US export controls, Reuters reported.

MITI said Malaysia won't let itself be used as a channel to get around export controls, and will act against anyone found breaking the law. Since July 2025, sending high-performance US AI chips on from Malaysia has needed a MITI permit.

## What happens next

- **Thursday, Dec 3:** Huang is due to testify in public before the US Senate Banking Committee. Lawmakers from both parties have questioned how Commerce handled the case and Huang's return.
