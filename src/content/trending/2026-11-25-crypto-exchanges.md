*2-minute read*

The Securities Commission Malaysia (SC) told The Star that trading crypto on a platform it hasn't registered may leave you with limited legal help. Its register lists five exchanges.

![Two hands holding a white smartphone with a blank blue screen.](/images/trending/2026-11-25/photo-crypto-exchanges-2026-11-25.jpg)

Exchanges are the apps and websites where people buy and sell cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin. The five on the SC's register, as of July 20, are HATA Digital, Luno Malaysia, MX Global, SINEGY DAX and Kinetic DAX. A platform licensed in another country still needs the SC's approval before it can operate here, the SC said, and one that's still applying counts as unregistered. So joining the queue doesn't count as getting in.

The SC's Investor Alert List includes Binance, the world's biggest crypto exchange, for operating a digital asset exchange without registration, and the SC took enforcement action against it in July 2021. The Star's questions followed a hack at Binance last month involving about US$6.3 billion.

On a registered exchange, the SC supervises the operator, your crypto has to be kept separate under its custody rules, and there's a way to settle disputes, with possible compensation where relevant, the SC said. It advises investors to use only registered exchanges and to check a platform's status before investing.

Experts told The Star the warning signs include promises of guaranteed profits and unsolicited messages on chat apps or social media. "If it sounds too good to be true, it usually is," said Prof Dr M. Selvakumar of Universiti Sains Malaysia's Cybersecurity Research Centre. He also warned that "recovery services" asking for fees upfront are often a second scam aimed at people who have already lost money. Think of it as the scam's sequel.
