*2-minute read*

The Employees Provident Fund (EPF) wants half its investments outside Malaysia within five to six years, up from 38% now, its chief executive told Bloomberg Television on Wednesday.

![The Earth seen from space, with Africa and Europe on the right and the Moon small in the dark on the left.](/images/trending/2026-11-25/photo-epf-overseas-2026-11-25.jpg)

If you work in the private sector, that's your money he's talking about. Your monthly retirement contributions go into EPF, which had RM1.55 trillion in investment assets at the end of September. So more of your savings could be getting a passport.

Investing overseas means buying shares, bonds and other assets in other countries. Chief executive Ahmad Zulqarnain Onn said the fund is making the shift at a time of higher share valuations and bond yields, and more uncertainty between countries. He also said the fund favours infrastructure, where AI, digitisation and the move to cleaner energy are driving a lot of spending.

Some of the money already has its passport stamped. From July to September, EPF's overseas investments earned RM13.68 billion, or 53% of the quarter's investment income, EPF said this month.

Overall, though, EPF's [income](/trending/2026-11-17-epf/) is growing more slowly. It grew just 3% that quarter from a year earlier, and he said then that he expects the slowdown to continue to the end of the year.
