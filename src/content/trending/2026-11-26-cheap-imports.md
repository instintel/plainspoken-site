*2-minute read*

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim says the government will consider stricter rules on cheap goods sold through overseas shopping apps, especially from China. The Cabinet has already agreed on steps to curb them slightly.

![Three sealed cardboard parcels on a doormat outside a front door.](/images/trending/2026-11-26/photo-cheap-imports-2026-11-26.jpg)

If your cart fills up on apps like Temu, there's already a tax on the small stuff. Since Jan 1, 2024, goods sold online for RM500 or less and shipped in from abroad carry a 10% sales tax. That's RM1 on every RM10 of goods.

Anwar said it on RTM's Dialog Negara on Wednesday night. A local brand's founder had asked how the government balances cheap goods on platforms such as Pinduoduo and Temu against local businesses. The influx, he said, is "killing smaller companies".

But nobody's locking the door. Anwar said the apps won't be blocked. They can come in if local companies are involved or if they guarantee quality and health, and anything labelled halal must really be halal. So the door stays open, just with a longer checklist taped to it.

No details yet on what the Cabinet's steps are or when they start. Anwar did have a word for shoppers, though: don't be won over by ads for goods whose quality isn't guaranteed. He called it "Babylon's conspicuous consumption", which is a very grand name for buying stuff you don't need because an ad told you to.

There's also a separate law in the works. In Budget 2027 last month, the government said it will table an E-Commerce Bill to make online platforms and sellers more accountable.
