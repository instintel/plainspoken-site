*2-minute read*

Most Malaysians think they can spot a scam, and most who come across one still engage with it. In a survey of 1,930 Malaysian adults earlier this year, 73% said they were confident they could recognise a scam, yet 70% of those who met one interacted with the scammer.

![A smartphone in a green case lying face up on the edge of a wooden table.](/images/trending/2026-11-28/photo-scam-survey-2026-11-28.jpg)

Confidence, it turns out, doesn't hang up the phone.

The survey is by the Global Anti-Scam Alliance (GASA), a nonprofit, and Malay Mail reported it on Saturday. Of those who interacted, 31% lost money, or personal information that led to a financial loss. Phone calls were the channel people reported most, at 69%, and WhatsApp led the apps, at 67%.

The police numbers put a ringgit figure on it. From January to October this year there were 56,802 online scam cases and RM1.56 billion lost, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail told Parliament on Wednesday. That's about RM5 million a day.

If you've already sent money, his advice is speed. Call the National Scam Response Centre (NSRC) on 997, so the police and Bank Negara Malaysia can flag or freeze the accounts involved. He called the first five minutes after a transfer the "golden hour", because the money can be moved through several mule accounts, bank accounts used to pass it along, in that time.

A golden hour that lasts five minutes. Scammers don't need long.
